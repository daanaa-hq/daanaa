# MERIT North Star

**Five-year vision. Refreshed annually. Holds steady between refreshes.**

---

## The vision

By 2031, MERIT is **civic infrastructure** for the US nonprofit sector:

- **Every IRS-registered 501(c)(3)** has an accurate, fair, current profile
- **Every donor** who wants to give thoughtfully has the tools to do so privately
- **The "invisible majority"** — orgs under $50K/year — are visible, supported, and equipped
- **A GPO-style vendor ecosystem** has measurably lowered the cost of running a small nonprofit (payments, office, marketing, insurance)
- **MERIT itself** is operated as a mission-locked entity — LLC or 501(c)(3) — with no perception-risk revenue streams and a clear succession plan

## What MERIT is

A trust layer for the nonprofit sector built on three things:
1. **IRS public data**, treated with care and fully attributed
2. **Privacy-first architecture** — donor data stays donor's data
3. **Deterministic, transparent scoring** — no proprietary black boxes

## What MERIT is NOT

- A payment processor (we never touch donor money)
- A charity rating service (we treat all 501(c)(3)s equally)
- An advocacy platform (we stay out of cause controversy)
- A donor identification service (privacy by architecture)
- A regulatory authority (we surface IRS data, we don't enforce)
- A consulting firm (we provide infrastructure, not advice)

## The four moats

1. **Privacy architecture** — built into the data model, not bolted on
2. **GPO vendor ecosystem** — network effects compound; incumbent rating sites can't easily copy
3. **Deterministic scoring** — transparent rules vs. opaque ratings build trust
4. **Mission lock** — non-negotiable principles in operating agreement prevent drift even under acquisition pressure

## Phase plan

### Phase 0: Directory + badges (Months 1–6)
**State:** Read-only public directory; no claims; no transactions; tip jar for MERIT itself.
**Goal:** Establish data, scoring, trust, brand. Get to 500 newsletter subscribers, first sector report published, first 100 user research conversations.

### Phase 1: Claims + nonprofit support (Months 7–18)
**State:** Nonprofits can claim profiles. Full Nonprofit Success department activates. Possibly form 501(c)(3) parallel to LLC.
**Goal:** 1,000 claimed profiles, first 3 advisors active, first sponsor confirmed, first sector report cited in mainstream sector press.

### Phase 2: GPO vendor ecosystem (Months 19+)
**State:** Vendor partnerships live; first GPO offers active; measurable cost savings tracked.
**Goal:** 5,000 claimed profiles, 10+ active vendor partners, $1M+ aggregate savings demonstrated, MERIT regarded as sector infrastructure.

### Phase 3: Sector infrastructure (Year 4+)
**State:** Open data APIs widely used by other civic-tech projects. Annual State of the Sector report. Foundation funding sustains operations.
**Goal:** Treated as the default trusted source for verified nonprofit data; quoted by sector press, used by funders, integrated into giving platforms.

## What we measure ourselves on

Three categories of metrics, in priority order:

### Trust metrics (most important)
- Nonprofits who say "MERIT got it right" when shown their profile
- Donors who say "I learned something useful"
- Sector journalists who cite MERIT data correctly
- Auditable transparency: every claim verifiable

### Engagement metrics
- Active nonprofits (logged in last 30 days)
- Active donors (used wallet in last 30 days)
- Sector report downloads/reads
- Newsletter open rates

### Mission metrics (Phase 2+)
- $ saved by nonprofits via GPO
- Time saved per nonprofit per month
- Small nonprofits (<$50K) served vs. total
- Geographic coverage of underserved areas

## What we explicitly do NOT measure ourselves on

- Donation volume processed (we don't process donations)
- Average donation size (not our business)
- Comparison rankings between nonprofits (we don't rank)
- Donor demographic profiling (privacy by design)
- Revenue per nonprofit (we don't charge nonprofits)

## The honest read

This is ambitious. Most projects at this stage don't make it five years. The reasons MERIT might:

1. **The mission is timeless** — the nonprofit sector's problems aren't going away
2. **The architecture is right** — privacy-first and IRS-grounded compounds trust over time
3. **The AI-augmented org model fits** — one founder + Claude can operate at a scale that wasn't possible 2 years ago
4. **The market is real** — Charity Navigator and Candid succeed; there's room for a third positioned differently
5. **The funder thesis is strong** — civic tech with privacy and transparency is exactly what mission-aligned funders want to support

The reasons it might not:

1. **Solo founder burnout** — addressed in operating rhythm but real
2. **Slower growth than expected** — directory is a long game
3. **Vendor terms or regulatory changes** — risk-managed but not eliminated
4. **Competitor moves** — possible Candid pivots
5. **Mission drift under funding pressure** — addressed by mission lock

The plan handles each of these. Execution determines the outcome.
