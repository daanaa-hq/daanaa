# The Daanaa Language & Mindset

The voice half of our infrastructure: how we talk about nonprofits to
encourage giving without
ranking, grading, or shaming anyone. This is the working companion to
STEWARDSHIP.md Principles 4 (small orgs deserve fairness) and 5 (we do not
weaponize transparency). Every skill, page, email, and AI output follows it.

---

**The vision this serves:** `VISION_GIVING_INFRASTRUCTURE.md` — Daanaa as
public infrastructure so every nonprofit can receive support and every person
can give easily. Language is part of that infrastructure: words that grade
close doors; words that invite open them.

## The core mindset

**Giving is the donor's generous act, not a vetting exercise.**

We are not a watchdog, a rating agency, or a scorecard. We are a discovery
layer that helps a person find work worth supporting and hand them off to the
organization itself. Everything we surface exists to *open a door*, never to
issue a verdict.

The test we run on every sentence: **the kitchen-table test.** Would this line
sound right said out loud, kindly, across a kitchen table — to a donor AND to
the founder of the small nonprofit being described? If either would wince, the
line is wrong.

## The language rules

### 1. Context, not verdicts
- Every signal is "a starting point, not a verdict."
- Financial data is *context within a peer group*, never a grade.
  A $200K community org is never compared against Kaiser.
- We removed percentile-movement tables from profiles because rank deltas
  mostly reflected OUR pipeline refreshes, not the org's trajectory —
  presenting that as "how they've tracked" would have been a quiet lie.

### 2. Additive framing only
- Signals raise visibility; they never lower dignity.
- There is no F-rating, no failure tier, no "falling behind," no red-flag
  iconography. If a signal can only subtract, we don't ship it.
- When we retired the lamp-tier badge from donor-facing profiles (2026-07-17),
  the reason was exactly this: "Spark" next to someone else's "Beacon" read as
  a grade despite our intent.

### 3. Need is an invitation, not a judgment
- The health signal vocabulary is HEALTHY / STABLE / NEED_SUPPORT — never
  "failing," "at risk," "struggling," or "underperforming."
- "Needs support" copy: *"Fewer than six months of operating reserves. An
  invitation to support, not a judgment of the work."*
- Discovery of thin reserves is a reason to give, framed as such.

### 4. Never imply what isn't true
- Never imply verification, ranking, or endorsement that doesn't exist.
- AI-discovered facts carry honest labels at the surface:
  *"Donation link found by AI, not yet confirmed by the organization."*
- Uncertain data says so: filing years on compensation figures, freshness
  dates on research, "beta" on unrefined features.

### 5. Words we avoid, and why

| Avoid | Because | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| rank, ranking, top-rated | Implies a contest between orgs | peer context, standing among peers |
| grade, score (as verdict) | Casts us as judge | financial context, signal |
| vet, scrutinize, "give wisely" | Casts orgs as suspects and donors as auditors | "give with heart" |
| failing, struggling, at-risk | Shames the org publicly | needs support |
| unverified (bare) | Reads as warning | "found by AI, not yet confirmed by the organization" |
| watchdog, exposé, accountability (as threat) | Weaponized transparency | transparency as context |
| neglected, falling behind (in outreach) | Pressure/shame framing | "your page is ready for you" |

### 6. Plain, human, warm
- Human-readable sentences. No hyphenated jargon ("mission-critical,
  donor-centric"). Few dashes. No corporate AI cadence — "even though it is
  made by Daanaa AI it does not have to sound like regular AI."
- Small orgs get the same care in copy as large ones: equal dignity is a
  writing rule, not just a scoring rule.

### 7. Donors are never watched
- We never reference an individual's giving history, wallet contents, or
  behavior in any copy, feature, or outreach. Giving is private by default.
- No social pressure mechanics, no public giving feeds, no leaderboards —
  for donors OR for orgs.

## Where this lives in the product

- **Peer grouping**: every comparison happens inside an archetype + revenue
  band cell, so likes are compared with likes (P4 structurally enforced).
- **Hidden gems**: small, financially healthy, low-profile orgs get surfaced
  *specifically because* attention pools around big names.
- **Mistake Registry**: every org page carries a visible corrections path —
  when we're wrong, we say so on the page itself (P6).
- **Hand-off model**: money never touches us; the ask always ends on the
  org's own channel (P8). Our language reflects a guide, not a gatekeeper.

## Where this lives in the workflow

- **comms-steward skill**: every outbound draft passes a three-read gate —
  factual honesty, then voice, then a dedicated pressure/shame/urgency read.
- **Board simulation**: donor-facing language changes get six-perspective
  review (including an ED seat that reads as the nonprofit being described).
- **Decision workflow**: any copy that ranks, nudges, or implies tracking is
  checked against STEWARDSHIP.md + PRIVACY-INVARIANTS.md before it ships.
