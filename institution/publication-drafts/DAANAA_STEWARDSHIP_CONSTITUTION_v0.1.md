# Daanaa Stewardship Constitution v0.1

Status: publication draft for review  
Authority: draft only; does not replace `institution/CONSTITUTION.md`, `institution/AUTHORITY.md`, adopted amendments, or founder rulings  
Prepared: 2026-07-14

## Preamble

Daanaa exists to make giving easy by strengthening the organizations that strengthen communities.

Daanaa holds information, tools, attention, relationships, research, and institutional authority as trusts. These powers are temporary. They must be exercised for the benefit of nonprofits, donors, volunteers, communities, and future stewards, not for extraction from them.

This Constitution is not a technical manual, privacy policy, legal terms document, or founder memoir. It is a decision framework. It preserves stable principles while allowing policies, tools, models, and operations to improve.

## Article I. Mission

1. Daanaa's mission is to make giving easy.
2. Daanaa serves the United States nonprofit community.
3. Daanaa strengthens nonprofit discovery, truthful context, nonprofit capacity, donor understanding, volunteer participation, shared learning, and responsible public-interest research.
4. Financial sustainability is a duty because an institution that cannot continue cannot steward well.
5. Financial sustainability serves the mission. The mission does not serve financial extraction.

## Article II. Authority as Trusteeship

1. Authority inside Daanaa is temporary trusteeship, not ownership of the mission.
2. Every person, agent, system, partner, or successor acting as Daanaa is accountable to the mission, adopted Charter, Constitution, stewardship principles, and approved governance records.
3. A human lead retains final accountable authority for institutional judgment unless and until a properly adopted governance structure changes that authority.
4. Accountable human authority must itself be governed by documented reasoning, review, succession planning, and limits against silent weakening of core protections.
5. No implementation choice may override a higher stewardship rule.

## Article III. Human Dignity and Mutual Respect

1. Nonprofits are not data objects, sales leads, or rating targets. They are mission-bearing institutions led by people with constraints, histories, and community obligations.
2. Donors are not behavioral inventory. Their generosity and interests must not be converted into pressure, manipulation, or undisclosed monetization.
3. Volunteers are not free labor to be extracted. Their time and dignity must be respected.
4. Mutual respect must be implemented as behavior: clear language, correction paths, consent where required, accessible explanations, non-shaming context, and proportionate transparency.
5. Daanaa must be understandable to small organizations with limited technical capacity, not only to sophisticated institutions.

## Article IV. Information as a Trust

1. Information entrusted to Daanaa must be handled as a trust.
2. Daanaa must distinguish public records, nonprofit-provided information, independently verified information, calculated context, estimates, AI-generated content, human-authored interpretation, unverified information, and unknown provenance where material.
3. Public government records may remain publicly visible.
4. Daanaa-created enhancements and nonprofit-provided information must be correctable and, where appropriate, removable, exportable, or withdrawable by the nonprofit.
5. Private nonprofit information must not be sold.
6. Donor activity and Wallet information must be minimized, protected, and excluded from sales, vendor targeting, public ranking, or pressure mechanisms.

## Article V. AI as Assisted Intelligence

1. AI may assist Daanaa. It may not replace accountable judgment.
2. AI may be unobtrusive, but never deceptively hidden.
3. Where AI materially affects public representation, access, burden, trust, or high-consequence interpretation, Daanaa must provide disclosure, reviewability, sources, assumptions, confidence, and uncertainty proportionate to consequence.
4. AI must not silently issue verdicts about nonprofit worth.
5. AI-generated drafts prepared from public information must be disclosed before nonprofit confirmation when used in concierge or profile-assistance workflows.
6. A human remains accountable for institutional use of AI outputs.

## Article VI. Context, Not Verdict

1. Peer Financial Context and similar systems exist to provide context, not verdicts.
2. Daanaa must not shame organizations for structural, developmental, or field-specific realities.
3. Scores, labels, sorting, visual hierarchy, and language must be reviewed for the way users actually experience them.
4. If a context feature functions as a rating in practice, it must be governed as a rating risk even if it is not described as one.
5. Public-facing claims about financial context must state limitations clearly.

## Article VII. Privacy and Data Minimization

1. Daanaa must collect only information reasonably needed for the stated purpose.
2. Entrusted information must have a defined purpose, owner, retention expectation, and correction or deletion path where applicable.
3. Donor activities are private unless the donor intentionally chooses otherwise.
4. Research must use minimum necessary data and must distinguish public records, aggregate data, consented data, and entrusted data.
5. Service providers may not become an excuse to weaken privacy promises.
6. Privacy controls must be testable where possible and honestly documented where not yet enforceable.

## Article VIII. Nonprofit Autonomy and Capacity Transfer

1. Daanaa must preserve nonprofit autonomy.
2. Tools should build capacity: after using Daanaa, a nonprofit should be more able to understand, explain, correct, export, or manage its own work.
3. Daanaa must avoid hidden dependence where the nonprofit can act only through Daanaa without understanding or control.
4. Nonprofit communications, donor relationships, and public identity remain controlled by the nonprofit except where public records are independently shown as public records.
5. Convenience does not justify lock-in.

## Article IX. Donation Boundary

1. Daanaa must not take custody of donations.
2. Daanaa must not process donations as merchant of record.
3. Daanaa must not take a percentage or cut of donations.
4. The donation relationship and transaction must remain directly between donor and nonprofit through the nonprofit's own channels.
5. Donation-adjacent tools, links, reminders, receipts, or records must be governed so they cannot blur this boundary.

## Article X. Commercial Independence

1. Payment, partnership, sponsorship, consulting, vendor relationships, or future financial services must not influence public truth, public visibility, ranking, search treatment, descriptions, recommendations presented as independent, or Peer Financial Context.
2. Optional paid services may exist only if clearly separated from public truth and core public access.
3. Daanaa must remain useful to nonprofits that cannot pay.
4. Vendor and consulting relationships require conflict review, documentation, and auditability.
5. Public language about free access must be precise enough to preserve mission-aligned sustainability without misleading users.

## Article XI. Research, Evidence, and Humility

1. Daanaa should learn from its work and share useful public-interest knowledge responsibly.
2. Research claims must distinguish evidence, hypothesis, aspiration, and principle.
3. Research using entrusted or sensitive data requires consent, minimization, safeguards, and clear publication thresholds.
4. Daanaa must be willing to correct findings, retire unsupported claims, and publish limitations.
5. Universal principles may be shared beyond the United States, but implementation must be locally adapted.

## Article XII. Correction and Accountability

1. Mistakes must be correctable.
2. Correction paths must be visible, respectful, and proportionate to harm.
3. Material corrections affecting public nonprofit representation should be logged in a way future stewards can review.
4. Daanaa must not hide errors to protect appearance.
5. Repeated correction patterns should feed back into product, policy, research, and governance.

## Article XIII. Institutional Memory and Succession

1. Daanaa's knowledge must not depend on one founder, one server, one vendor, one format, one model, or one undocumented workflow.
2. Binding decisions, authority rules, policies, public promises, recovery procedures, and major risks must be preserved in durable institutional records.
3. A successor should be able to understand what exists, why it exists, what is authoritative, how to recover it, and what requires constitutional review.
4. Backup failure, access concentration, undocumented credentials, and founder-only administration are stewardship risks.
5. Institutional memory must include what Daanaa changed its mind about.

## Article XIV. Change, Amendment, and Gap Review

1. Daanaa must be able to change without quietly weakening its mission.
2. Stable principles belong in constitutional documents. Adaptable rules belong in policies. Evolving details belong in implementation.
3. Amendments affecting mission, Charter promises, privacy, AI governance, donation custody, paid influence, public scoring, data ownership, or succession require explicit constitutional review.
4. Conflicts between documents must be surfaced, not silently resolved by convenience.
5. Constitutional gaps must be recorded with responsible owner, review trigger, and founder or board decision requirement.

## Article XV. What Daanaa Refuses To Become

Daanaa refuses to become:

- A toll booth between generosity and nonprofits.
- A seller of nonprofit vulnerability.
- A hidden rating system disguised as context.
- A platform where payment buys public truth or attention.
- A donor surveillance system.
- An AI system that represents nonprofits without accountability.
- A consultancy firewall that exists in language but not in practice.
- An institution that depends on one person's memory to remain ethical.
- A system that treats legal compliance as the ceiling of stewardship.

## Article XVI. Future Generations

Daanaa should be built so future stewards inherit more than code. They should inherit a coherent mission, protected principles, honest records, working controls, clear unresolved questions, and the freedom to improve implementation without losing the reason the institution exists.

The work continues.
