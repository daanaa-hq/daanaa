## 1. The Principle, Stated

**Every Daanaa interaction should leave an organization more capable than it was before the interaction.**

Every service, feature, call, letter, and tool answers one question before it ships and again after it runs:

> *What can this organization now accomplish independently that it could not before?*

If the honest answer is "nothing — but it will keep coming back to us," the service has produced dependence, not capacity, and it fails this principle no matter how useful, how popular, or how profitable it is.

The corollary is stated in the charter and repeated here because it is the heart of the matter: **long-term dependence is never an objective.** Organizations should continue using Daanaa because it continually creates new value — never because they can no longer function without it. An institution that becomes indispensable to the people it serves has quietly inverted the relationship of stewardship: it has made the sector serve its continuation.

## 2. Why This Is a Distinct Principle

Library 006 already establishes that Daanaa's work is capacity, and defines eleven dimensions of it. What 006 measures is capacity *added*. This principle measures something 006 does not: capacity *retained when Daanaa steps back* — the difference between a crutch and a strengthened leg.

A service can add capacity and create dependence at the same time. A donation-receipt service that produces perfect letters forever, while the organization never learns what a compliant receipt requires, has added capacity by one reading and subtracted it by another: the organization now has a new single point of failure, and it is us. The charter names this failure mode directly: *avoid features that increase dependence without increasing capacity; avoid becoming indispensable; build systems that help others become stronger.*

## 3. The Seven Measures

Each interaction or service is assessed on the measures the charter commissions. None is a target to maximize; per 006, they are read together, like vital signs.

1. **Knowledge transferred.** Did the organization learn the shape of the task — could its people now explain it to a successor? (Echoes 004's confidence-building principle: the measure of success is not that the machine did the work, but that the person would face the task next time with less dread.)
2. **Administrative burden removed.** Hours returned to mission, per organization, per year — with the burden *staying* removed, not merely relocated into managing us.
3. **Financial capacity.** More money arriving, less leaking: working verified donation paths, documented cost reductions, findability by donors. Never money handled — Article XI is untouched by this principle.
4. **Volunteer capacity.** Willing hands connected to work; matches that survive the first meeting.
5. **Governance maturity.** Boards found and strengthened; the organization more likely to survive its founder.
6. **Decision quality.** Decisions made with evidence the organization can cite — peer context in a grant application, benchmark data in a board vote — rather than guesswork or expensive consultants.
7. **Community impact enabled.** The downstream chain of 006: capacity becoming meals, tutoring, rebuilt trails. Slowest to measure, easiest to inflate; claimed only where it can be shown.

**A note the founder must resolve:** these seven measures and 006's eleven dimensions overlap heavily but are not identical (006 adds time, AI access, procurement, research, banking access, collaboration as distinct dimensions; the seven fold several of these together). The institution should not run two capacity vocabularies. The steward's recommendation is that the eleven dimensions describe *where* capacity lives, and these seven measure *whether it transferred* — a where-list and a whether-test, cross-referenced in both documents. This reconciliation is a founder decision and is flagged in the 2026-07-11 library review.

## 4. Applied to Real Daanaa Services

**Concierge onboarding call.** The template case. The AI drafts the profile; a human confirms it with the founder in about ten minutes. Capacity-transfer test: after the call, the founder should know what her public record says, why it says it, and how to change it herself — the call teaches the shape of the task while removing its burden. It transfers knowledge (measure 1) and removes burden (measure 2) in the same ten minutes. Failure mode to watch: a call so smooth the founder learns nothing and must call again for every future change. The closing minute of the script should hand over the controls, not just the result.

**Donation-receipt letter service (concept, pending legal footing).** The highest-risk service under this principle, precisely because it is recurring by nature — receipts happen every year. Passing the test means: correct letters produced *and* the organization shown what makes them correct (the required elements, the deadline, the records to keep), so that using Daanaa remains a convenience rather than becoming a necessity. The honest measure is whether an organization that stopped using the service tomorrow could produce a compliant receipt. If the answer is no after two years of service, we have built dependence and billed for it.

**Group purchasing (concept, under exploration).** Aggregated buying is structurally collective — no small organization can hold wholesale pricing power alone, so "independent accomplishment" cannot mean solo replication. The transfer here is different in kind: organizations learn what things should cost, what terms are negotiable, and what questions to ask any vendor — knowledge that survives even if the purchasing group dissolves. Failure mode: a purchasing program whose pricing is opaque teaches nothing and merely swaps retail dependence for Daanaa dependence. Independence constraints from Article XII apply in full: no vendor may buy position.

**Profile claiming.** Already close to the principle's shape: claiming converts an organization from a described object into an author of its own record, with versioned attestations. Each claim should leave the organization knowing what the public record system is, what provenance its statements carry, and how corrections work — literacy in its own public existence. Failure mode: a claim flow completed by clicking through, understood by no one.

## 5. The Honest Tension with Sustainable Revenue

This section exists because pretending the tension away would violate Article II.

Most recurring-revenue businesses are, structurally, dependence engines: retention is the metric, switching costs are the moat, and a customer who no longer needs you is churn. This principle deliberately forbids the moat. Daanaa commits to making its own services easier to leave — and Article XI simultaneously insists the institution maintain the means to continue, because "a mission that cannot pay for itself becomes a memory."

These commitments can coexist, but only under a specific theory of revenue: **organizations pay, or are paid for, because Daanaa keeps creating *new* value — never because leaving is hard.** Concretely:

- Revenue may come from *recurrence of need* (each year has new receipts, new filings, new donors) but never from *manufactured lock-in* (proprietary formats, hostage data, knowledge withheld to stay necessary).
- The exit must stay open by design: an organization's data exportable, its knowledge transferred, its record portable. If churn is low, we must be able to show it is low because of ongoing value, not exit cost.
- Some capacity transfer will genuinely reduce revenue — a well-taught organization buys less help. The institution accepts this the way a good teacher accepts graduation. What fills the gap is scale (there are hundreds of thousands of small organizations, most not yet reached) and the B2B layer that exists precisely so the public mission does not have to pay for itself by leaning on nonprofits.
- Where this tension bites on a specific pricing decision, the mission wins, per Article X — and the decision and its cost are logged, per Article VI, so future stewards can see what the principle actually cost and judge whether it earned its place.

What this section does not claim: that the tension is resolved. It is managed, decision by decision, in the open. An Emerging Principle that claimed to have solved the oldest problem in mission-driven revenue would be exhibiting exactly the false certainty the charter forbids.

## 6. What This Principle Does Not Mean

- It does not mean abandoning organizations once "capable" — capability is not a graduation ceremony, and new needs arrive continuously.
- It does not mean refusing to do things *for* organizations. Doing-for is often the right first gift (004: "start with a gift, not a demand"). The principle governs the trajectory: doing-for should trend toward doing-with, which should trend toward they-can.
- It does not forbid recurring services. It forbids recurring services whose recurrence depends on the customer's ignorance.
- It does not override equal dignity: an organization that *chooses* ongoing help, with open eyes and an open exit, is exercising capacity, not lacking it.

## 7. Maturation: Evidence That Would Earn Stewardship Status

Per the charter, this principle earns constitutional status only through repeated evidence. The evidence that would justify promotion:

1. **Convergence.** Independent research efforts and service retrospectives repeatedly reach this conclusion unprompted — the charter's own test for Emerging Principles.
2. **Measured transfer across at least three services.** The seven measures instrumented and showing, over multiple years, organizations accomplishing independently what they previously could not (e.g., self-service profile updates after concierge calls; compliant receipts produced in-house after service use).
3. **Survived a revenue conflict.** At least one documented decision where the principle cost real revenue and was upheld, and the institution remained sustainable — evidence the principle is livable, not just admirable.
4. **Exit behavior observed.** Organizations that left Daanaa's services demonstrably retained the capacity gained — the leg stayed strong without the crutch.
5. **Usefulness to a successor.** A future steward, applying the principle to a service that does not exist today, finds it decisive rather than decorative.

## 8. Falsification: Evidence That Would Kill It

An Emerging Principle must state what would prove it wrong. This one is falsified if sustained evidence shows:

1. **Transfer does not happen.** Organizations do not, in practice, retain capability — knowledge decays with volunteer turnover faster than any service can transfer it, and "capacity transfer" is revealed as a comforting description of ordinary service delivery.
2. **Organizations do not want it.** Small nonprofits, honestly surveyed, consistently prefer done-for-them service over taught-to-them capacity — and treating that preference as a defect disrespects the very people the principle claims to serve (Article VIII cuts both ways).
3. **The measures corrupt.** The seven measures become targets, get gamed, and start punishing the organizations they were meant to serve — the distortion 006 warns of, arriving anyway.
4. **It is unaffordable.** No revenue theory consistent with §5 sustains the institution, and the choice becomes the principle or the mission's survival — at which point Article X governs, and the principle, not the mission, is what yields as a constitutional candidate.

If falsified, this document is reclassified Deprecated with reasons recorded, per the charter: never delete important reasoning.

## 9. Open Questions

- Who administers the seven measures, and how do we measure "knowledge transferred" without burdening organizations with our surveys? (Measurement without a steward is a known Constitutional Gap.)
- How does the principle apply to *donors* — should every donor interaction also leave the giver more capable of giving well, and is that the same principle or a sibling?
- Does capacity transfer have a floor — organizations so small or transient that transfer is impossible and doing-for is simply the honest service?
- Reconciliation of the seven measures with 006's eleven dimensions (founder decision, §3).

The work continues.
