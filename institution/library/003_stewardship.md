# THE DAANAA STEWARDSHIP CONSTITUTION

**Authority:** Stewardship Library Elaboration
**Parent:** `STEWARDSHIP.md`
**Related:** `institution/CONSTITUTION.md`, `institution/IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`
Library Document 003 · First Draft · Version 1.0 (Living Document)
Drafted 2026-07-11 under founder brief

This is not a legal document. It is the moral and institutional operating
system of Daanaa: the principles that remain true regardless of what tools
exist, who leads, or what the world looks like when it is read. It binds
every person and every automated system acting on Daanaa's behalf.

## Article I — Stewardship

Everything Daanaa holds — information, trust, capability, resources,
reputation — is held in stewardship, not owned. A steward's question is
never "what may we do with this?" but "what were we entrusted with this
for?" Stewardship outranks ownership, convenience, growth, and pride. Every
role in the institution, from the newest volunteer to the most capable
machine, is a stewardship before it is anything else.

## Article II — Trust

Trust is the institution's only durable asset, and it cannot be stored. It
is earned continuously or it decays. Every claim Daanaa makes must be
supported by evidence someone could examine; where evidence is weak,
incomplete, or old, that weakness is stated plainly. Nothing is presented as
more certain than it is. An institution that inflates its certainty spends
trust it has not earned.

## Article III — Information

Information about others carries responsibility toward them. Whoever is
described by information has a stake in its accuracy and its dignity.
Information is gathered only for the mission, kept only while it serves,
attributed to its source, and corrected without defensiveness when found
wrong. The smallest organization's record is kept with the same care as the
largest's.

## Article IV — Reasoning Systems

Daanaa will always use the most capable instruments of its era, including
systems that reason, learn, and act. Such systems increase human capability;
they never reduce human responsibility. A machine may draft, sort, suggest,
and explain. A human answers for what is done. No automated system is beyond
question, beyond correction, or beyond being switched off; and none may be
tuned, openly or quietly, to serve any interest other than the mission.

## Article V — Privacy

The decision to give is intimate. Daanaa protects it structurally, not
merely by policy: systems are built so that private acts of generosity
cannot be exposed, performed, pressured, or traded upon — so that a breach
of this principle requires breaking the system, not merely bending a rule.
Daanaa collects the least it can, keeps it the shortest time it can, and
never treats a person's giving as material for anyone else's purposes.

## Article VI — Transparency

Daanaa explains itself. Methods are published. Significant decisions are
recorded with their reasons, so that those who come later can understand,
audit, and disagree. Errors are acknowledged where they occurred, corrected
promptly, and documented rather than hidden. Transparency about failure is
the price of being believed about anything else.

## Article VII — Continuous Improvement

The institution assumes it is wrong somewhere, always, and goes looking.
Every practice, including this constitution, is open to improvement in the
open. Principles may be strengthened at any time; they may never be diluted
silently, for convenience, for growth, or for money. What cannot change is
recorded here precisely so that everything else can change safely.

## Article VIII — Human Dignity

Every person Daanaa touches — a donor of great means, a volunteer of great
heart, the unpaid founder of the smallest organization, a stranger with a
question — is met with the same respect. Daanaa does not shame, does not
rank human worth, does not weaponize what it knows, and does not design
systems that humiliate, pressure, or manipulate. Small is not lesser.
Struggling is not failing.

## Article IX — Mutual Respect

Daanaa serves people of every culture, faith, and worldview, and borrows
wisdom from all of them without belonging to any. It does not take sides in
politics. Disagreement, inside or outside the institution, is met with
argument, not contempt. The institution speaks calmly even when spoken to
otherwise.

## Article X — Mission Before Self-Interest

When the interests of Daanaa and the interests of the mission diverge, the
mission prevails. No revenue, partnership, ranking, or advantage may be
bought at the mission's expense; no partner, funder, or friend may purchase
influence over what Daanaa reports as true. If ever the institution's
survival requires betraying its purpose, the purpose is kept and the
institution accepts the consequence.

## Article XI — Financial Sustainability

Poverty is not a virtue in an institution; dependence is not either. Daanaa
maintains the means to continue — earned honestly, spent carefully,
disclosed openly — because a mission that cannot pay for itself becomes a
memory. But sustainability serves the mission; the mission is never resized
to serve sustainability. Daanaa never stands between a giver and the
receiver of a gift, and never takes custody of what passes between them.

## Article XII — Institutional Independence

Daanaa's judgments are its own. No outside party — commercial, governmental,
philanthropic, or personal — may direct its findings, its visibility, or its
standards, by pressure, payment, or access. Independence is protected in
structure, not just in intention, so that it survives leaders who are
tempted and times that are hard.

## Article XIII — Future Generations

Daanaa is built for people who are not yet born, who will inherit both its
obligations and its records. Every generation of stewards leaves the
institution more capable, better documented, and easier to continue than it
found it: decisions explained, knowledge preserved, succession prepared.
The measure of each generation is what the next one can do because of it.
