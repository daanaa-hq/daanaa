Board members enter the Zoom room. Chair has printed the memo. Investor has highlighted revenue section. Privacy advisor has a list of questions on sticky notes.

*(Pause. Murmurs of acknowledgment.)*

## OPENING REMARKS (Founder) — 5 minutes

**Founder:** "Thanks for making time for this. I know student service is a bigger lift than nonprofit dashboards. But here's the framing:

We built Daanaa to help people discover nonprofits. That's working. But discovery is one moment. Then what? Someone finds an org, they visit the website, they maybe donate once. We don't have a reason for repeated engagement.

Student volunteering changes that. Students need places to serve. Nonprofits need students. Schools need verification they can trust. And we have the infrastructure: volunteer hours system, nonprofit dashboards, authentication, stewardship framework.

This isn't a pivot. It's an application of existing infrastructure to a new user segment.

I'm asking for authorization to run a 90-day pilot in Houston with 3-5 schools and 20 nonprofits. If it works, we have a new product line. If it doesn't, we've learned something at low cost and contained risk.

The memo covers scope, privacy, safety, resource allocation, and—because this is Daanaa—explicit Charter guardrails on revenue and partnerships. Those guardrails are non-negotiable, and we're putting them in writing so future boards can point to them.

Questions?"

*(Pause.)*

---

## BOARD CHAIR — 8 minutes

**Chair:** "I've read the memo twice. I want to start with resource allocation because it's the most concrete thing.

You say 3 FTE during development, 2.5 during pilot. Where are you pulling that from? Are we slipping core Daanaa roadmap?"

**Founder:** "Product manager: reallocating 0.5 from content roadmap. We're deprioritizing the insights dashboard—pushing it to Q4.

Engineer: Full-time during development sprint (6-8 weeks). Then half-time during pilot for support/iteration.

Designer: 0.3 from existing capacity (nights and weekends if needed, honestly, but we'll try to find slack).

Yes, we slip core roadmap by 4-6 weeks. Insights dashboard goes from July launch to November. Is that acceptable?"

**Chair:** "November is pushing it into year-end review cycle. But I can live with that if the pilot signals are strong. What's 'strong'?"

**Founder:** "By week 9, we need: at least 2 schools participating, at least 10 nonprofits committed, at least 50 students enrolled. If we hit 30 students, that's the hard floor—board decides at that point if we continue or pause."

**Chair:** "Okay. And exit criteria. When do you recommend pulling the plug?"

**Founder:** "Three conditions: (1) enrollment stays below 30 by week 15, (2) significant fraud detected, (3) COPPA/FERPA review surfaces blocking issues. Any of those, we pause and reassess."

**Chair:** "Good. Clear thresholds. Next question: Houston. Why not Austin or Dallas where you already have relationships?"

**Founder:** "Houston is stronger demographically for a pilot. Diverse nonprofit ecosystem, known to me, established relationships with school partners through previous work, mid-size market—not too small, not too large for learning. Austin and Dallas are backups if Houston timing doesn't work."

**Chair:** "Fair enough. Last thing: the Charter guardrails. You added explicit language about revenue models and vendor partnerships. Why that wording?"

**Founder:** "Because I wanted the board to know that future management won't silently monetize core service or trade student data for partnership. Charter Promise #5 says core platform stays free. Charter Promise #3 says student data isn't a commodity. I'm locking those in now so you don't have to re-litigate them with future boards."

**Chair:** "That's the right move. Privacy Advisor, you have concerns?"

---

## PRIVACY/LEGAL ADVISOR — 12 minutes

**Privacy Advisor:** "I have three things.

First: COPPA. The memo says 13+ for pilot, no under-13 without legal clearance. Good boundary. But here's what I need to know: Can a 13-year-old's parent override the student's choice to participate? Or delete their record?"

**Founder:** "Good question. For 13+, the student consents independently—parental notification but not consent needed under COPPA. For under-13 (if we expand later), parental consent required and parent can override/delete. Week-2 legal review will document this precisely."

**Privacy Advisor:** "Okay. That's standard. Document it so we have a clear audit trail.

Second: The vendor firewall. You added language: 'No student data sharing with vendors or commercial partners, regardless of partnership depth.' I like it. But I need to know how you operationalize this. What if AmeriCorps wants to know how many students we've served? Do we give them aggregate numbers?"

**Founder:** "Aggregates only. 'Daanaa's pilot partner with 5 Houston nonprofits, serving 120+ students in Q3.' No individual-level data. We're happy to share impact metrics for PR, not personal records."

**Privacy Advisor:** "Put that in your Charter compliance doc. Aggregates only. No individual-level data sharing regardless of the partnership value. That's your hedge against data-trading pressure."

**Founder:** "Done. That's going in week 2 documentation."

**Privacy Advisor:** "Third: Data deletion. You say students can delete records. What about the nonprofit supervisor who verified the hours? Do they lose their verification?"

**Founder:** "Student deletes their personal record—name, age, school affiliation gone. But the audit log remains: 'Verification #1234 was revoked on [date]' so we don't create gaps in our own compliance documentation. No PII in the audit log, just the event."

**Privacy Advisor:** "Good. That protects you legally if there's ever a dispute. Alright, I'm satisfied. You're taking data seriously."

**Chair:** "Thank you. Education Expert, your turn."

---

## EDUCATION/YOUTH SECTOR EXPERT — 10 minutes

**Education Expert:** "I love this in principle. Students need places to volunteer. Schools need reliable records. But I have two concerns.

First: Will schools actually participate? Houston ISD is massive. Charter schools are nimble. Where are you starting?"

**Founder:** "I have existing relationships with two charter school networks in Houston. Both have expressed interest informally. I'm not counting on ISD district office—too slow. But the charters can move fast, and if they're successful, ISD notices and follows. That's how education changes often work."

**Education Expert:** "Okay. That's realistic. Second concern: You mentioned 13+ only for pilot. Are we locking out middle school students? That's a big market."

**Founder:** "Yes, we're being conservative. 13+ captures high school + some middle school 8th graders. It's about 60% of the student volunteer market. Pilot is learning—if 13+ works, we propose under-13 expansion to the board in month 4 or 5, informed by legal review. But we're starting safe."

**Education Expert:** "I appreciate that. One more thing: comparison to other platforms. How is this different from VolunteerHub or iServe?"

**Founder:** "Those are volunteer-management tools built for nonprofits. We start with discovery—students find orgs via Daanaa, see their mission and community impact. Then we add service tracking. We're unique in connecting students to 1.7M organizations and linking it to nonprofit operations. They're tools for nonprofits. We're tools for students discovering the nonprofit ecosystem."

**Education Expert:** "That's a real distinction. I'm in. Next board member?"

**Chair:** "Nonprofit Operations Director, you had concerns in the prep notes."

---

## NONPROFIT OPERATIONS DIRECTOR — 15 minutes

**Nonprofit Ops:** "I'm skeptical. Service verification sounds simple until it's not. Let me walk through a scenario:

Student shows up at nonprofit, works with Supervisor Jane. Student logs 5 hours. Jane approves. Month later, Jane has left the nonprofit. New leadership says those hours shouldn't have been approved. Student disputes. What happens?

**Founder:** "That's a real scenario. Here's our process:

1. Student can dispute within 30 days of verification.
2. Our team reviews the dispute—we have audit logs showing what Jane approved.
3. If Jane left but acted appropriately, we side with the student.
4. If Jane violated policy, we flag it with nonprofit leadership.
5. If pattern emerges (Jane rejected everyone), that's a red flag for audit.

We also do random audits of 5-10% of submissions, independent of disputes."

**Nonprofit Ops:** "Okay. That's reasonable. But here's my real concern: operational burden. You say nonprofits click to approve. How long does that actually take?"

**Founder:** "Our UI design shows: Student name, hours claimed, activity description. Supervisor clicks 'Confirm' or 'Reject' with optional comment. We estimate 2-3 minutes per submission. During pilot, we're measuring this closely. If it's 10 minutes per submission, design is broken."

**Nonprofit Ops:** "And what if a nonprofit gets overwhelmed? 'We have 200 students asking to volunteer'?"

**Founder:** "Pilot is sized to prevent that. Max 5-10 students per nonprofit in first semester. If demand is higher, that's success—we help them scale or recruit more supervisors. But we start small."

**Nonprofit Ops:** "Last thing: liability. If a student claims hours they didn't work, who's liable? The nonprofit?"

**Founder:** "Nonprofit bears liability for their supervisor's verification. We're the record-keeper. Our nonprofit agreements include standard indemnification language. Nonprofit's insurance covers volunteer-related claims. We maintain audit trail so disputes are documented, not hidden."

**Nonprofit Ops:** "You need to have those agreements drafted and vetted before enrollment. Non-negotiable."

**Founder:** "Week 2-3, those are drafted. We review with counsel before any school recruitment starts."

**Nonprofit Ops:** "Good. Alright, I can support this."

**Chair:** "Thank you. Impact Investor?"

---

## IMPACT INVESTOR — 18 minutes

**Impact Investor:** "Two things. First: business model. You mention freemium, subscription, school contracts. But the Charter says never charge for the platform. How do we reconcile that?"

*(This is the moment the Charter guardrail language matters.)*

**Founder:** "Great catch. You noticed we added specific language in the memo. Let me read it:

'Core student service (discovery, logging, verification, certification) remains free. Only advanced features beyond the core service may be monetized, and any such change requires explicit board authorization to maintain Charter compliance.'

So core platform stays free, always. Freemium means: core features free, premium reporting or analytics paid. Subscription means: schools can pay for enhanced dashboards with custom reports, but basic student verification is free. School contracts means: contracts for support/implementation, not for access.

The key: board gates any monetization. We don't decide in isolation. And we don't weaken Charter Promise #5."

**Impact Investor:** "Okay, that's the guardrail I needed to hear. But let me be blunt: will boards 5 years from now honor this? Or will they say 'times have changed'?"

**Founder:** "That's why we're documenting this now. Future boards can say 'We considered monetization on [date], voted to keep core free, and that's recorded in our charter compliance log.' If future management proposes to weaken it, anyone can pull this memo and say 'Remember what we committed to?'"

**Impact Investor:** "Document that conversation log. Make it part of your governance record."

**Founder:** "Will do."

**Impact Investor:** "Second question: scale potential. If this works in Houston, what's the path to 50 cities?"

**Founder:** "Depends on network effects. Year 1: Houston proof-of-concept. If successful, we pitch to school districts and education nonprofits in Texas, Southwest. Year 2-3: regional expansion if adoption metrics are strong. Year 5: national potential if partnership networks (AmeriCorps, Points of Light) see value.

But here's the honest part: this only scales if schools and nonprofits actually want it. If Houston pilots with 3-5 schools and 20 nonprofits and adoption is weak, we don't have a product. We iterate or discontinue."

**Impact Investor:** "Cost structure?"

**Founder:** "Pilot phase: high overhead amortized over small base. Estimate $10-15 per verified hour. At scale: $1-3 per hour (mostly staff audits, dispute resolution). Existing platforms charge $5-20 per hour. We could be competitive."

**Impact Investor:** "What's your timeline to break-even on student service?"

**Founder:** "Honestly? 18-24 months post-launch if we scale to 5-10 cities. If we stay Houston-only or hit adoption headwinds, maybe never. This isn't a venture play. It's a mission play. We build it if it serves students and nonprofits, even if margins are thin."

**Impact Investor:** "That's the answer I needed. Alright. One last thing: I like that you added the Charter language. Makes me more confident you're not going to compromise for 'growth' pressures."

**Founder:** "That's exactly why it's there."

**Impact Investor:** "I'm in."

**Chair:** "Thank you. Founder, is there anything else before we vote?"

## FOUNDER CLOSING STATEMENT — 3 minutes

**Founder:** "Just one thing. This proposal comes from a genuine problem we see: students need places to serve, nonprofits need volunteers, schools need verification. And we have the infrastructure to solve it without breaking Charter principles.

But I want to be explicit: this is exploratory. We're not committing to national expansion. We're not committing to forever-free. We're committing to running a tight pilot, collecting data, and letting the board decide what's next.

If at week 9 adoption is weak, we pause. If legal review surfaces issues, we delay. If fraud emerges, we shut down. The board maintains control.

And the Charter guardrails aren't just rhetoric. They're structural. Revenue decisions go through the board. Partnership decisions require data-firewall compliance. Age policy has a hard boundary until legal says otherwise. That's how we stay true to founding principles.

I'm asking for authorization to proceed."

**Chair:** "Thank you. Let's vote."

3. Early termination clause if enrollment falls below 30 students or significant fraud is detected

5. Post-pilot evaluation and board decision before any material expansion or major certification expenditures
6. Charter Promise #5 compliance: core student service remains free; board must approve any advanced feature monetization
7. Charter Promise #3 compliance: no student data sharing with vendors or commercial partners; partnerships are operational only

All in favor?"
