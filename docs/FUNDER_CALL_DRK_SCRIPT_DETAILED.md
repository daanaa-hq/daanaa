## The Call

### Opening (1.5 min)

---

### The Problem (2 min)

> "Here's what we're solving: Philanthropy has a matching problem. On one side, you have 2+ million nonprofits competing for attention. On the other, you have donors trying to understand where their dollars actually create impact.
>
> The result? Smaller nonprofits—often the most innovative, most rooted in their communities—are invisible. Not because they lack impact. Because they lack size, brand, and administrative polish.
>
> Donors default to household names. Nonprofits default to funding survival, not mission excellence.
>
> **This is a systems problem, and it requires systems infrastructure.**"

---

### Our Solution (3 min)

> "Daanaa is infrastructure for nonprofit discovery, built on evidence. We index 1.87 million 501(c)(3) organizations from public IRS data. We assign each a peer financial context score—how they're doing relative to orgs like them in their mission space—and surface that transparently.
>
> We also surface 'hidden gems': small, financially healthy, low-profile organizations that donors would never find through Google. We do this algorithmically—no human curation, no paid placement.
>
> The result: Donors see mission impact, not size. Small nonprofits get discovery they deserve.
>
> **And critically: All of this is free, open, and independent.** No partner can pay to boost their visibility. No nonprofit is disadvantaged by lack of polish. The infrastructure is neutral."

---

### Why DRK (2 min)

>

## Likely DRK Questions

**"How is this different from Guidestar, Charity Navigator, etc.?"**

> "Those platforms use financial ratios and efficiency metrics. We use peer financial context—how is this organization doing relative to mission-matched peers in their peer group? It's not 'good' or 'bad'—it's 'here's your context.'
>
> More importantly: We don't rank or rate. We show evidence. No algorithm decides which orgs are 'best.' That's the donor's call."

**"How do you measure success?"**

> "Three metrics:
> 1. **Discovery fairness:** Small orgs see 2–3x more donor interest than they would through traditional search
> 2. **Transparency:** 1.87M orgs indexed, cause/location/financial health filters live, no paid placement
> 3. **Governance:** Stewardship principles are published, verified quarterly, and strengthened (not weakened) over time
>
> We'll publish these metrics in our impact report—not just growth metrics."

**"Won't vendors try to game the system?"**

> "Yes. That's why independence is structural, not aspirational. 
>
> Here's how it works:
> - Scores compute from public IRS data, algorithmically, with no human curation
> - No vendor can pay to change their score
> - No vendor can pay to boost visibility
> - If a vendor tries to influence rankings, it's a breach of their partnership agreement and grounds for removal
>
> The Stewardship Commitment makes this public. You can audit us."

**"What about your revenue model?"**

> "Future question—we haven't solved it yet. But any model we adopt can never compromise the core: discovery must stay free, fair, and evidence-based.
>
> Some ideas we're exploring: premium services for nonprofits (letters, compliance tools), not-for-profit transaction infrastructure (year 2+), licensed data for research.
>
> All of these are additive—they don't touch the discovery layer."

**"How do you prevent donor privacy erosion?"**

> "Structurally. We don't track giving behavior. We don't build profiles. Our Giving Wallet holds bookmarks and intent—favorites, nothing more. We never sell or share donor data. Donors can delete everything anytime.
>
> This is in our Stewardship Commitment and audited quarterly. If we ever shift, you can call us out."
