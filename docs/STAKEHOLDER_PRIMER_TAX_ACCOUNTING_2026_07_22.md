## Your Key Decisions

### Decision 10: Long-Term Revenue Model ⚠️ **VOTE NEEDED**

**Question:** How does Daanaa sustain itself? Free forever, freemium, B2B ESG, or hybrid?

**Your angle:** Tax and accounting structure of each model. Are we staying 501(c)(3)? Going B2B LLC?

| Model | Tax Structure | Your Question |
|-------|---|---|
| **Forever Free** | 501(c)(3), grant-funded | Can we fundraise sustainably? Funder tracking? |
| **Freemium** | 501(c)(3), mixed revenue | Does school tuition count as UBI (unrelated business income)? Tax on premium fees? |
| **B2B ESG** | LLC (separate from 501(c)(3)) or 501(c)(3) with UBI? | Is B2B revenue arm separate? Allocation of overhead? |
| **Hybrid** | 501(c)(3) core + LLC B2B arm | Complex but cleanest. Firewall? Shared services costs? |

**Tax Flags to Raise:**
- If freemium: Premium tier revenue needs UBI (unrelated business income) treatment. May trigger UDAP (unrelated debt-financed property) rules.
- If B2B ESG: Separate legal entity (LLC) is safest to avoid UBI scrutiny. Requires allocation of shared costs (office, staff).
- If hybrid: Need careful cost accounting between 501(c)(3) arm and LLC arm. IRS scrutinizes pass-throughs.

**Recommendation:** Hybrid (B2B LLC arm separate from 501(c)(3)). Clearest tax treatment, no UBI ambiguity, protects nonprofit status.

### Decision 13: Data Retention & Privacy Policy ⚠️ **VOTE NEEDED**

**Question:** How long do we keep volunteer records? Permanent, 7-year, 1-year, or hybrid PII + aggregates?

**Your angle:** Tax record retention obligations and FERPA/COPPA implications.

| Option | Retention Model | Tax/Accounting Impact |
|--------|---|---|
| **Permanent** | Keep all records forever | No compliance conflict, but privacy risk |
| **7-Year** | PII delete after 7 years | Aligns with IRS audit statute (7 years). Safe. Recommended. |
| **1-Year** | Aggressive deletion | Risky for tax documentation audit; may violate IRS doc retention |
| **Hybrid** | PII 7 years, aggregates forever | Best of both: tax-safe + privacy-first |

**Tax Flags to Raise:**
- IRS requires 7-year record retention for substantiation (charity records). If we delete before 7 years, we lose audit trail.
- If we receive government grants (Title I, AmeriCorps, etc.), federal agencies may require 7+ year record retention.
- Aggregate data (totals, not PII) can safely stay indefinite (no tax compliance conflict).

**Recommendation:** Option D (Hybrid). PII deleted after 7 years (IRS-safe), aggregates retained forever (no tax downside, enables research).

### Decision 9: Donor Account Structure (Adjacent) ⚠️ **ADVISORY**

**Question:** Should household accounts use Head of Household model with co-givers, or individual profiles?

**Your angle:** Tax filing simplicity, joint filing support, consolidated receipts.

| Model | Filing Complexity | Joint Filing Support | Dependent Coordination |
|--------|---|---|---|
| **Single** | High (separate per person) | Poor (each person gets separate receipt) | Poor (kids' giving separate from parents) |
| **Head of Household + Co-Givers** | Low (one receipt per HoH) | ✅ Perfect (joint filing = one receipt) | ✅ Good (kids' giving rolls up to parent) |
| **Household Entity** | Very Low (entity level) | ✅ Best (entity filing = one receipt) | ✅ Excellent (all under one entity) |

**Tax Filing Benefit (Filing Jointly with Dependents):**
- **Traditional model:** Akbar gives $500, spouse gives $300, kids give $100 → 3 separate receipts at tax time
- **Head of Household model:** Family gives $900 total → **One consolidated receipt to HoH**
  - Easier tax prep: accountant enters one charitable line item (not three)
  - Simpler reconciliation: one receipt matches one line on return
  - Natural fit: joint filers (1040 MFJ) have one HoH who signs return

**Tax Compliance:**
- Head of Household = person filing the 1040 (who signs the return)
- Spouse filing jointly? Receipt goes to HoH (who signs return jointly)
- Dependents give? Income flows to HoH's return (per tax law)
- Result: One receipt per tax-filing household unit

**Recommendation:** Head of Household + co-givers. **Reduces tax prep complexity for households.** One receipt per filer = fewer reconciliation errors, cleaner accounting.
