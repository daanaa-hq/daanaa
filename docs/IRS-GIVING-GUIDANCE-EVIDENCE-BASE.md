# Daanaa Ways to Give: IRS Guidance Evidence Base

**Purpose:** This document compiles authoritative IRS guidance on charitable giving methods to support Daanaa's Ways to Give initiative. Every claim about tax deductibility and substantiation requirements links to IRS official publications and guidance.

**Compiled:** 2026-07-26  
**Stewardship Principles Aligned:** P1 (Mission before growth), P3 (Trust signals evidence-based), P5 (No shame language), P8 (Never handle funds)

---

## I. IRS PUBLICATIONS & CORE FRAMEWORKS

| Publication | Topic | Key Guidance | IRS Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Publication 526 (Charitable Contributions)** | Universal deductibility requirements | To deduct a charitable contribution, you must generally itemize deductions on Schedule A (Form 1040). **New for 2026:** If you do not itemize, you may deduct up to $1,000 ($2,000 if filing jointly) of cash contributions to certain qualified organizations. | [Pub 526 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526) |
| **Publication 526** | Qualified organizations | Include nonprofit groups that are religious, charitable, educational, scientific, or literary in purpose, or that work to prevent cruelty to children or animals. | [Pub 526](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526) |
| **Publication 561 (Valuation)** | Fair market value definition | Deductible amount equals the fair market value (FMV) of property at time of donation — the price the property would sell for on the open market. | [Pub 561 (12/2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p561) |
| **Publication 561** | Appraisal thresholds | If donated property exceeds $5,000, a qualified appraisal is required (except publicly traded securities). | [Pub 561](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p561) |
| **IRC § 170** | Deduction limits | Charitable deduction generally limited to 60% of AGI (cash contributions), 50% for noncash, 20% or 30% in some cases. Excess carries forward to subsequent years. | [Charitable contribution deductions](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/charitable-contribution-deductions) |
| **IRC § 170(e)** | Appreciated securities (Section 170(e)(1)(B)(i)) | Holding period: more than 12 months required for full FMV deduction on appreciated securities. If held ≤ 12 months, deduction limited to cost basis. | [Form 8283 Instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8283) |
| **Topic 506** | Charitable contributions quick reference | IRS Topic 506 provides summary reference for all charitable giving rules. | [IRS Topic 506](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc506) |

## II. GIVING METHODS — WHAT THE IRS EXPLICITLY SAYS

### A. Cash & Check Donations

| Method | Tax Treatment | Substantiation Required | Deduction Limits | IRS Position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cash/Check/Direct Payment** | Fully deductible (no appreciation discount) | Bank record (canceled check, bank statement, credit card statement showing org name, date, amount) OR written communication from charity. Any amount. | Up to 60% of AGI (cash contributions) | [Substantiating Charitable Contributions](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/substantiating-charitable-contributions) |
| **Monetary contributions of $250+** | Same as above | **Required:** Contemporaneous Written Acknowledgment (CWA) from charity containing: (1) amount of cash, (2) description of property (if any), (3) statement of whether goods/services received, (4) description and value of any goods/services. CWA must be obtained by tax filing date. | Up to 60% of AGI | [Charitable contributions: Written acknowledgments](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/charitable-contributions-written-acknowledgments) |

**Key Caveat (Stewardship Principle #3 - Evidence-Based):** The bank record rule changed in 2006. Written records prepared by the donor (personal notations, check registers) are **no longer sufficient** — third-party documentation required.

### B. Appreciated Securities (Stocks, Bonds, Mutual Funds)

| Method | Tax Treatment | Holding Period | Valuation | Substantiation | IRS Position |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Publicly traded securities (FMV + appreciation)** | Deductible at full FMV; donor receives long-term capital gains tax benefit (no capital gains tax on appreciation) | > 12 months required for full FMV deduction | FMV = average of highest and lowest quoted selling prices on valuation date | Form 8283, Section A (if ≤$5K) or Section A even if >$5K for publicly traded. No appraisal required. | [IRC § 170(e)(1)(B)(i)](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8283); [Publication 561](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p561) |
| **Appreciated securities held ≤12 months** | Deductible at **cost basis only** (no appreciation benefit); still avoids capital gains tax on the appreciation | ≤ 12 months | FMV at donation date | Same as above | [Form 8283 Instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8283) |

**Implication for Daanaa Ways to Give:** Daanaa can note that securities donations are among the most tax-efficient giving methods for appreciated assets, and link to IRS guidance without needing to provide valuation advice (that's the donor's broker's role).

### C. Noncash Property (Household Items, Clothing, Vehicles, Art, Real Estate)

| Property Type | Deductibility | Special Rules | Substantiation (≤$5K) | Substantiation (>$5K) | IRS Position |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Household goods/clothing** | Deductible only if in "good used condition or better" (post-Aug 17, 2006 rule) | Exception: paintings, antiques, art, jewelry, collections NOT subject to condition rule | CWA required if $250+ | Qualified appraisal + Form 8283 Section B | [Publication 561](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p561) |
| **Books, artwork, antiques** | Deductible at FMV | FMV determined by comparable sales analysis; art often requires appraisal | CWA required if $250+ | Qualified appraisal (generally required for >$5K) | [Publication 561](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p561) |
| **Real estate** | Deductible at FMV (can include donated conservation easements) | Complex rules vary by easement type | CWA required if $250+ | Qualified appraisal required | [Publication 561](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p561) |
| **Vehicles** | Deductible at FMV | Special rules if charity sells vehicle; if sold, donor deduction limited to gross proceeds | Form 1098-C filed by charity | Qualified appraisal if claimed >$5K | [Publication 561](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p561) |

**Note for Daanaa:** Daanaa should not help donors value noncash property — direct them to nonprofits, appraisers, or tax advisors. Daanaa's role is linking to the charity's donation portal or EIN-based router.

### D. Digital Assets & Cryptocurrency

| Method | Tax Treatment | Holding Period | Deduction Amount | Reporting | Appraisal | IRS Position |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cryptocurrency/Digital assets held >1 year** | Deductible at FMV on date of donation | > 1 year | FMV at donation date (same as appreciated securities benefit) | Noncash contribution on Form 990-N/990 by charity; Form 8949 by donor if claimed >$500 | Required if >$5K claimed | [Digital assets FAQs](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/frequently-asked-questions-on-digital-asset-transactions) |
| **Cryptocurrency held ≤1 year** | Deductible at **lesser of cost basis or FMV** (same as short-term securities) | ≤ 1 year | Cost basis (more conservative) | Same as above | Same as above | [Digital assets FAQs](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/frequently-asked-questions-on-digital-asset-transactions) |

**Implication for Daanaa:** DAF platforms and donation platforms increasingly accept crypto. Daanaa's guidance should note: "Crypto donations are treated like appreciated securities for tax purposes. IRS requires contemporaneous written acknowledgment and valuation on the donation date."

### E. Recurring/Automatic Gifts

| Method | Tax Treatment | Substantiation | Timing | IRS Position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Recurring automatic contributions (monthly, annual)** | Each individual payment treated as separate contribution; must be made by Dec 31 of tax year to be deductible that year. Recurring setup does not defer tax year. | Each payment ≥$250 requires CWA from charity (not covered by initial CWA). Donors must keep records for each payment. | Payment date determines tax year, not setup date. Year-end auto-withdrawals must clear bank by 12/31 to count. | [Publication 526](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526); [Substantiating Charitable Contributions](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/substantiating-charitable-contributions) |

**Implication for Daanaa:** Daanaa can encourage recurring gifts as a convenient giving method, but must note that donors should confirm each year's contributions are deductible on that tax year's return. Nonprofits should provide annual CWAs.

### F. Volunteer Time & Out-of-Pocket Expenses

| Item | Deductibility | Special Rules | Substantiation | IRS Position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Volunteer hours/time** | **NOT deductible** (ever) | Value of volunteer time cannot be claimed as a charitable deduction, even if donor loses income | N/A | [Publication 526](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526) |
| **Out-of-pocket expenses (volunteer-incurred)** | **Deductible if:** (1) paid by volunteer, (2) not reimbursed by charity, (3) directly related to volunteer work | Examples: gas/mileage to volunteering site (standard mileage rate), materials purchased for charity project, uniform/equipment required by charity | CWA required if $250+; itemize expenses on Schedule A | [Publication 526](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526) |

**Implication for Daanaa Volunteer Feature:** Daanaa can surface volunteer opportunities via partner discovery, but must NOT frame volunteer hours as "gifts" or suggest they're tax-deductible. Out-of-pocket expenses are deductible separately from the volunteer time.

### G. Donor-Advised Funds (DAFs)

| Aspect | IRS Rule | Tax Treatment | Substantiation | IRS Position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Initial contribution to DAF** | Full deduction in year contributed (not when distributed) | Deductible at FMV at donation date | CWA required; must confirm sponsoring org has **exclusive legal control** | [IRS: Donor-Advised Funds](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/donor-advised-funds) |
| **DAF distributions** | Donor can recommend distributions; org has final say | NOT a deductible event (deduction claimed at initial contribution, not distribution) | Org tracks distributions; no additional CWA needed for each distribution | [Requirements for Donor-Advised Funds](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/requirements-for-donor-advised-funds) |
| **Qualified organizations for DAF contributions** | Can contribute to DAF sponsored by 501(c)(3) or community foundation | Contributions to war veterans' orgs, fraternal societies, or cemeteries' DAFs are NOT deductible | Standard charitable org verification via Tax Exempt Organization Search | [IRS: Donor-Advised Funds](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/donor-advised-funds) |

**Key IRS Concern (Stewardship Principle #3):** IRS is aware of organizations that abuse DAF mechanics to generate questionable deductions or provide economic benefits to donors/families. Legitimate DAF sponsors operate with full transparency.

**Implication for Daanaa:** Daanaa can link to DAF platforms as a giving method and note that DAF contributions are immediately tax-deductible (unlike the distributions). Important: Daanaa is NOT handling funds; the DAF sponsor does.

### H. Employer Matching & Workplace Giving Platforms (Benevity, CyberGrants, etc.)

| Scenario | Tax Treatment | Deductibility | Substantiation | IRS Position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Employee contribution + employer match** | Both the employee's gift AND the employer's matching contribution are separately deductible to the respective parties (employee deducts their gift, employer deducts match as business expense if structured properly) | Employee: deductible if to qualified 501(c)(3). Employer: deductible if structured as charitable contribution (not employee benefit) | Each party keeps separate records; CWA required for gifts ≥$250 | [Publication 525](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p525) (taxable/nontaxable income); [Sample matching contribution article](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-utl/OC-Matchcontributions.pdf) |
| **Payroll deduction** | Deductible in the tax year the payroll withholding occurs (each paycheck is a separate contribution) | Up to 60% of AGI (cash contributions) | Bank records (payroll stubs from employer showing charity donations) | [Publication 526](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526) |
| **Employer matching programs** | Employer matching is **voluntary and not required by law**. No specific IRS "match requirement" — employers set their own policies. | Employer donation counted as employer charitable contribution (if structured correctly). Employee's gift is separate. | Both keep records; charity issues CWA to employee for employee's gift | [Publication 526](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526) |

**Implication for Daanaa:** Daanaa can note that workplace giving is effective for employee donors. No specific IRS barriers. Link directly to nonprofit's workplace giving portal or EIN-based router.

## III. SUBSTANTIATION & DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

### Donor Responsibilities (What Donors Must Keep)

| Contribution Amount | Cash/Check | Appreciated Securities | Noncash Property | Form Requirements |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Any amount** | Bank record (canceled check, bank/credit card statement showing org name, date, amount) | Same | For $250+, need CWA | N/A (for <$250 cash) |
| **$250–$5,000** | Bank record PLUS CWA from charity | Same | CWA required. For noncash, CWA must describe property and estimate value | Form 8283, Section A (if noncash >$500 total claimed) |
| **>$5,000** | Bank record PLUS CWA from charity | Bank record PLUS CWA; no appraisal needed for publicly traded securities | CWA + Qualified Appraisal + Form 8283 Section B (signed by qualified appraiser and charity) | Form 8283 Section B attached to return |

**Source:** [Substantiating Charitable Contributions](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/substantiating-charitable-contributions); [Form 8283 Instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8283)

### Nonprofit/Charity Responsibilities (What Charities Must Provide)

| Donation Type | CWA Content | Signing Authority | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **All donations ≥$250** | Must include: (1) amount of cash, (2) description of property (no value unless goods/services received), (3) statement of goods/services received (if any), (4) description and value of those goods/services | Charity authorized official | Must be provided by date donor files return (contemporaneous) |
| **Noncash >$5,000** | Same as above PLUS qualified appraisal attached | Qualified appraiser signs appraisal; charity signs Part V of Form 8283 Section B | Same timing |
| **Quid pro quo contributions >$75** | Disclosure statement (separate from CWA) explaining which portion is deductible and FMV of goods/services provided | Charity official | Before or with solicitation |

**Implication for Daanaa:** Daanaa's website should note: "Ask your nonprofit for a contemporaneous written acknowledgment (receipt) if your donation is $250 or more. Nonprofits are required to provide this."

### IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search

Donors can verify if an organization is qualified 501(c)(3) before donating:
- **Tool:** [IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/tax-exempt-organization-search)
- **Implication:** Daanaa can link to this tool and note that only organizations listed here (or certain other categories) qualify for tax deductions.

**Source:** [Other eligible donees](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/contributors/other-eligible-donees); [Charitable organizations](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations)

## IV. SPECIAL RULES & CONSTRAINTS

### A. Quid Pro Quo Contributions (Goods/Services Received)

| Rule | IRS Position | Example | Implication for Daanaa |
|---|---|---|---|
| **If you receive a benefit in exchange for donation** | Deductible amount = donation minus FMV of benefit received | Donate $100, receive $40 concert ticket → deductible = $60 | Daanaa should note: "If a nonprofit gives you a t-shirt or other item for your donation, ask them for its value to reduce your deduction." |
| **Charity disclosure requirement (>$75 quid pro quo)** | Charity must provide written disclosure statement explaining (1) portion that's deductible, (2) FMV of goods/services. Penalty: $10/contribution, max $5K per event, if not disclosed. | Example: charity gala with $150 donation but $50 meal value → org must disclose $100 deductible | Daanaa's copy should clarify: "Some nonprofit fundraisers include events or items. The deductible portion is only the excess over fair market value." |
| **Intangible religious benefits exception** | No disclosure needed if org provides only "intangible religious benefits" (e.g., prayers, access to religious services) | Church donation | Daanaa should link to publication for clarity. |

**Sources:** [Charitable contributions: Quid pro quo contributions](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/charitable-contributions-quid-pro-quo-contributions); [Substantiating charitable contributions](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/substantiating-charitable-contributions)

### B. Disqualified Organizations (Cannot Receive Tax-Deductible Gifts)

| Organization Type | Tax Deductibility | Example | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|
| **501(c)(4) civic leagues, social welfare orgs** | NOT deductible | League of Women Voters, some advocacy orgs | [Tax Exempt Organization Search](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/tax-exempt-organization-search) |
| **501(c)(5) labor organizations** | NOT deductible | Unions | Same |
| **501(c)(6) business leagues, chambers of commerce** | NOT deductible | Local chamber of commerce | Same |
| **War veterans' orgs (specific rules)** | Generally deductible (with exceptions for certain veteran groups) | VFW, American Legion | IRS has updated rules; verify with Tax Exempt Search |
| **Fraternal societies** | Generally NOT deductible | Masonic lodges (though some charitable arms are deductible) | Same |
| **Private individuals, political candidates, political parties** | NOT deductible | N/A | N/A |

**Implication for Daanaa:** Daanaa's registry should note: "Search the IRS Tax Exempt Organization database to confirm tax-deductibility before giving. Only 501(c)(3) orgs and certain other categories qualify."

**Source:** [Other eligible donees](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/contributors/other-eligible-donees); [Donations to Section 501(c)(4) organizations](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/other-non-profits/donations-to-section-501c4-organizations)

### C. AGI Limits on Charitable Deductions

| Contribution Type | Limit | Carryover |
|---|---|---|
| **Cash contributions** | 60% of AGI | Excess carries forward up to 5 years |
| **Noncash property (from capital gains)** | 30% of AGI (for appreciated securities, etc.) | Carryforward applies |
| **Noncash property (from basis)** | 50% of AGI | Carryforward applies |
| **Qualified conservation contributions** | Up to 100% of AGI (special case) | Carryforward allowed |

**Implication for Daanaa:** High-net-worth donors should know that very large gifts may exceed AGI limits for a single year (but carryover resolves this). Daanaa should note: "Very large gifts may exceed this year's deduction limit, but the excess carries forward. Consult a tax advisor."

**Source:** [Charitable contribution deductions](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/charitable-contribution-deductions); [Publication 526](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526)

## V. ADVANCED GIVING METHODS

### A. Donor-Directed Funds & Intermediaries

| Method | Tax Treatment | Key IRS Rule | Risk/Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Donation routed through intermediary (e.g., DAF, EIN-based router)** | Donor deducts gift to intermediary (if intermediary is qualified 501(c)(3)); distribution to final charity is not a second deductible event | Intermediary must be a qualified org with "exclusive legal control" over assets. IRS scrutinizes chains that don't add value. | IRS is aware of abuse patterns (sham pass-throughs). Legitimate intermediaries (DAFs, community foundations) have clear governance. Daanaa itself is not a pass-through intermediary. |
| **Pass-through (conduit) foundations** | Donor deducts contribution to foundation if foundation meets 50% AGI deduction limit (vs. 30% for other private foundations) | Foundation must distribute 100% of contributions received within 15 months of receipt year. | Requires careful accounting and timing; primarily used by large donors. |

**Implication for Daanaa:** Daanaa is a **discovery platform**, not an intermediary. When Daanaa links to a nonprofit or EIN-based platform, the donor's gift goes directly to the charity (or through a legitimate platform like PayPal Giving Fund or a DAF). Daanaa never holds or routes funds.

**Source:** [Private pass-through foundation](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/private-foundations/private-pass-through-foundation); [Donor-advised funds](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/donor-advised-funds)

### B. Charitable Remainder Trusts (CRTs) & Retained-Life-Interest Gifts

| Method | Tax Deduction | Income Tax Treatment | Appraisal/Reporting | IRS Complexity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Charitable remainder trust (CRT)** | Partial deduction (remainder interest only); deduction = FMV of property minus present value of annuity/stream to donor | Income from trust distributed to donor annually and taxed to donor at ordinary income rates (then capital gains, then corpus). | Form 5227 filed annually by trust. Complex actuarial calculations required. | High — requires trust attorney and tax advisor. Not a simple giving method. |
| **Retained-life-interest / gift annuity** | Deduction = donation minus present value of annuity using Section 7520 rate and actuarial tables | Donor receives annuity; portion taxed as ordinary income, portion as return of basis. | No Form 8283; treated as investment income to donor. | High — requires actuarial valuation. |

**Implication for Daanaa:** Daanaa should **not** explain CRTs or life-interest gifts in detail. These are advanced tax strategies. Link to:
- [Charitable remainder trusts](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-remainder-trusts)
- Tax advisors and nonprofit gift planning officers

**Source:** [Charitable remainder trusts](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-remainder-trusts)

## VI. STEWARDSHIP-ALIGNED COPY GUIDANCE

### What Daanaa Can Say (Evidence-Based)

- "Donations to IRS-qualified 501(c)(3) organizations are tax-deductible. [Link: verify at IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search]"
- "Cash donations require a bank record (receipt, canceled check, or credit card statement). For gifts of $250 or more, ask the nonprofit for a written acknowledgment."
- "Appreciated securities and cryptocurrency donations may receive more favorable tax treatment than cash. [Link: IRS guidance on appreciated assets]"
- "Volunteer hours are not tax-deductible, but out-of-pocket expenses you pay for volunteer work are. [Link: IRS Publication 526]"
- "Workplace giving is available through your employer's payroll system. Each paycheck deduction is a separate contribution."
- "DAF (donor-advised fund) contributions are tax-deductible in the year you give, not when the fund distributes the money."

### What Daanaa Must NOT Say (Violates Principles #3, #5)

- "Double your impact with our matched giving program" ❌ (employer matching is optional, not guaranteed)
- "You can deduct your volunteer time" ❌ (not true; violates P3 evidence-based)
- "100% of your donation goes to the charity" ❌ (platform fees apply; unless 100% is true)
- "Tax-deductible status guaranteed" ❌ (IRS determines status, not Daanaa)
- "Hidden financial problems make these orgs better deals" ❌ (violates P5 no-shame language)

### What Daanaa Should Not Provide (Outside Scope)

- Tax advice (donors should consult tax advisors)
- Valuation of noncash property (appraisers' role)
- Specific deduction calculations (CPAs' role)
- Legal advice on trust structures (attorneys' role)
