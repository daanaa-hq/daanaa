# Public Financial Context for Nonprofits

## What Daanaa is learning from public records

**Research brief**  
**August 2026**  
**Status:** Working paper for public and community review

## Why this work matters

People who want to support a nonprofit often begin with public information. They may find an organization’s mission, location, tax status, and financial filings. That information can be useful, but it is rarely complete and it is not always easy to compare.

A small community organization may have fewer filings, fewer staff, and less administrative capacity than a large national institution. That difference in public information does not tell us which organization has more value or stronger community relationships.

Daanaa is studying a more careful approach. We present financial information as peer context. We do not present it as a rating, a judgment, or a measure of mission impact.

## What V6 does

V6 looks for organizations that can reasonably be compared. Depending on the available public record, the comparison may consider:

* nonprofit category;
* state or region;
* funding pattern;
* revenue range;
* filing year;
* size of the available peer group.

When an organization has usable direct information and a sufficient peer group, the page can show direct financial context. When direct information is missing but comparable public records exist, the page can show peer context and say clearly that the information describes similar organizations rather than the organization itself.

When the evidence is too limited, Daanaa should show less. A missing signal means that the public record does not support a responsible calculation. It does not mean that the organization is weak or less deserving of support.

## What the current local snapshot shows

The local research snapshot contains 1,740,806 organizations. V6 has context placement for 1,737,032 organizations, or 99.8 percent of that snapshot.

The current placement groups are:

These are descriptive counts from a dated local snapshot. They are not proof that the method is accurate, fair, or complete. The database, application, and published explanation still require reconciliation before these figures become a final public claim.

## What V6 does not tell us

V6 does not measure mission impact, community trust, leadership quality, program effectiveness, or the likelihood that an organization will succeed. It does not establish a universal reserve target. A reserve level may reflect growth, restricted funds, a recent emergency, a different funding model, or a deliberate operating choice.

Financial research supports this caution. Studies of nonprofit reserves show that appropriate levels vary with organizational size, subsector, revenue volatility, and other conditions. A single threshold would create false certainty.

## Why smaller organizations need special care

Public data is not equally detailed for every organization. Smaller organizations may file simplified returns or have fewer years of comparable information. Rural organizations and organizations working in unusual fields may also have smaller peer groups.

Daanaa is testing five safeguards:

1. Compare organizations with relevant peers rather than with the entire nonprofit sector.
2. Label peer context as inferred when it is not based on the organization’s own direct information.
3. Withhold numeric comparisons when the evidence is too sparse.
4. Report coverage by organization size, geography, cause, and filing type.
5. Give organizations a clear way to report errors and explain unusual circumstances.

These safeguards are design commitments and research questions. They are not evidence that every group is already treated equally. Daanaa must test the results and publish what it finds, including results that challenge the method.

## What we are testing next

Daanaa has published an interactive research explorer at `/research/explorer` so users can inspect aggregate patterns by cause area, state, revenue range, filing year, and context group. The explorer shows the date and denominator for each view, provides a table alongside every chart, and explains what the data cannot show.

The next research tests will examine:

* whether smaller organizations receive comparable discovery visibility;
* whether direct and inferred context are understood by donors;
* how stable peer groups remain when thresholds change;
* whether missing public information is concentrated in particular sectors or regions;
* whether the public page displays the same values stored in the research database.

## Our standard for trust

Daanaa will earn trust by showing its work and its limits. That means identifying sources, dates, peer definitions, and uncertainty. It means correcting errors in public view. It means keeping payment, partnerships, and donor activity separate from search treatment and financial context.

Daanaa is not asking readers to accept V6 because it is new or technically complex. We are inviting nonprofit leaders, researchers, donors, and communities to challenge it.

The most accurate description today is simple:

> V6 is a developing method for presenting nonprofit financial context from uneven public records. It is intended to help people ask better questions, not to decide which organizations deserve support.

## Invitation to review

If you lead a nonprofit, study nonprofit finance, work with donors, or use public nonprofit data, we welcome criticism of the method. We are especially interested in where the approach may confuse public visibility with organizational quality, place an unnecessary burden on smaller organizations, or communicate more certainty than the evidence allows.
