# Attorney Call Script — Daanaa Legal Consultation
## 15-Minute Nonprofit + Tax Law Consultation

---

## OPENING (30 seconds)

**Greeting & Context:**

"Hi [Attorney Name], thanks so much for taking the call. I'm Akbar Khowaja. I'm the founder of Daanaa — it's a discovery platform that helps donors find and research nonprofit organizations. We index public IRS data, assign a peer financial context score to each org, and surface them through a searchable directory.

We're not handling donations ourselves — we're a hand-off layer. But as we're growing, I want to get ahead of three legal questions before we scale. We're being very cautious about structure and regulatory exposure. Is this a good time to dig in?"

---

## QUESTION 1: ENTITY STRUCTURE (5 minutes)

**Setup:**

"Let's start with how we're organized. Right now I'm running two entities: EcoMargins LLC, which is the for-profit operating company, and we're exploring a fiscal sponsorship model for Daanaa — the nonprofit discovery side — under an existing 501(c)(3).

The thesis is: EcoMargins handles B2B services to nonprofits — things like compliance tools, donation letters, visibility tooling — and that funds the Daanaa platform, which stays free and ad-free for donors.

**Question 1a:**
Given that structure — a for-profit parent (EcoMargins) funding a free discovery platform through a fiscal sponsor — what are the tax and liability implications we should think about? Are there red flags in that model?"

**Pause for response. Listen for:**
- Unrelated Business Income Tax (UBIT) concerns
- Fiscal sponsor liability or restrictions
- Alternative structures (direct 501c3, hybrid, etc.)

---

**Question 1b (follow-up, if not addressed):**

"And if we go direct 501(c)(3) instead — skipping the fiscal sponsor — what would we be giving up? I'm asking because I want to understand the trade-off between simplicity and control."

**Pause for response.**

---

**Question 1c (optional, if relevant):**

"One more thing on this: if EcoMargins stays for-profit and Daanaa is 501(c)(3), how strict is the boundary between them? Can the same team work on both, or does that create issues?"

**Pause for response.**

---

## QUESTION 2: CHARITABLE SOLICITATION REGISTRATION (4 minutes)

**Setup & Context:**

"Okay, second big one. Daanaa displays donation links — but here's the important part: *the nonprofits provide those links to us*. We don't discover them, we don't negotiate them, we don't get a commission. We're basically surfacing what each org has already published, plus a few programmatic discovery partnerships we've verified for accuracy.

The question is: by surfacing those links on our platform — making it easier for donors to give — are we 'soliciting' charitable contributions in a way that triggers state charitable solicitation registration?

**Question 2a:**
Does displaying org-provided donation links trigger CA or NY charitable solicitation registration requirements? Or does it matter whether we're doing it as a platform operator vs. as a nonprofit ourselves?"

**Pause for response. Listen for:**
- Definition of "solicitation" in CA/NY/other states
- Safe harbors for information platforms
- Whether being free + nonprofit-focused changes the analysis

---

**Question 2b (follow-up):**

"And if it does trigger registration — even in just one state — what's the practical lift? How many states do we need to worry about?"

**Pause for response.**

---

**Question 2c (optional, clarifying):**

"If a nonprofit *directly* provided a link to us — like, they submitted it through a form on our site — does that change anything? I'm trying to understand where the line is between 'we're publishing nonprofit data' and 'we're running a solicitation.'"

**Pause for response.**

---

## QUESTION 3: GROUP PURCHASING ORGANIZATION (GPO) MODEL (4 minutes)

**Setup & Context:**

"Last one. This is more forward-looking. We're exploring offering a Group Purchasing Organization discount code to nonprofit partners down the line. The idea: nonprofits could use the code to get a discount on, say, compliance software or donation-processing tools from vendors we've vetted.

We wouldn't take a cut — the vendor and the nonprofit would have a direct relationship. We're just the matchmaker. But I want to understand the liability and regulatory shape of that before we even pitch it.

**Question 3a:**
If we operate a GPO discount program for nonprofits — no commission to us, fully transparent, vendor relationships direct — what are the liability and regulatory risks we should plan for? Are there GPO-specific regulations we're crossing into?"

**Pause for response. Listen for:**
- GPO licensing or registration requirements
- Antitrust or price-fixing exposure
- Insurance requirements
- Disclosure/transparency standards

---

**Question 3b (follow-up):**

"And if we *do* want to eventually take a small commission — like 2–5% from the vendor for facilitating the relationship — how does that change the analysis? What kind of disclosures or agreements would we need?"

**Pause for response.**

---

**Question 3c (optional, if antitrust was mentioned):**

"On the antitrust side: we're aggregating nonprofits and offering them group discounts. Does that create any exposure, or is it fine as long as we're transparent about it and we're not steering them toward preferred vendors?"

**Pause for response.**

---

## CLOSING (2 minutes)

**Summarize & Next Steps:**

"Those are the three. I know that's a lot, so let me recap what would help us most:

1. A high-level view of whether our current structure — EcoMargins LLC + 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsorship — makes sense, or if there's a better way
2. Clarity on the charitable solicitation question — do we need to register in CA/NY/others, and if so, what's the practical path?
3. A rough sense of the GPO risks so we can plan accordingly

What would a next step look like? Would it make sense to do a deeper memo on one or more of these, or should we just get on a video call with you and [CFO/COO if applicable] to walk through the whole model?"

**Pause for response.**

---

**Budget & Timeline:**

"And one last thing: ballpark, what's your thinking on scope and timeline? We're not in a huge rush, but we want to get ahead of this before we scale the Daanaa discovery side or start talking to funders."

**Pause for response.**

---

**Closing Line:**

"Perfect. Thanks for taking the time. I really appreciate the thoughtfulness here — we want to do this right, and having a good legal foundation early makes a huge difference."

## ATTORNEY PROFILE — WHO TO CALL

**Ideal match:**
- California or national practice (with CA/NY expertise)
- 5+ years nonprofit tax law + 501(c)(3) formation
- Experience with:
  - For-profit/nonprofit hybrid structures
  - Charitable solicitation regulations
  - Group purchasing / vendor management programs
- Ideally: founder-friendly or early-stage tech nonprofits

**Where to find:**
- Lawyers.com / Justia (filter: nonprofit tax)
- Local bar association referral (CA State Bar, NYC Bar)
- Nonprofit networks (National Council of Nonprofits, AFP chapter)
- Recommendations from fiscal sponsor orgs you're considering
