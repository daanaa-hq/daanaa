# Academic and Community Methods Review Plan

Date: 2026-07-18
Status: proposed, local-only

## Purpose

Obtain independent critique of Daanaa’s peer financial context methodology,
especially for nonprofits whose public financial information is missing,
partial, delayed, or structurally unavailable.

This is a critique request, not an endorsement request. No academic or
community partner should influence an individual nonprofit’s score, ranking,
visibility, or treatment.

## Core question

What can Daanaa responsibly communicate when it does not have enough public
financial evidence to calculate a reliable context signal?

## Non-negotiable design principles

- Missing data is not evidence of poor financial health.
- No public financial context score when minimum evidence requirements are not met.
- “Insufficient public evidence” must be distinct from financial concern.
- Public records, nonprofit-provided information, and inference remain separate.
- Any nonprofit-provided information is optional, labeled, controlled by the nonprofit, and never sold.
- Uncertainty, source date, coverage, and limitations must be visible to donors.
- Missingness must be measured by organization size, age, geography, cause, and operating model.

## Review questions

1. Are the current indicators financially meaningful and interpretable?
2. What minimum evidence threshold is defensible for a public context signal?
3. Does data availability systematically favor larger or more resourced nonprofits?
4. Are peer groups too small or heterogeneous for reliable comparison?
5. How should stale, conflicting, or incomplete filings be handled?
6. Do donors understand the difference between no data, not scored, and concern?
7. Could the presentation shame or suppress smaller community-rooted organizations?
8. What sensitivity, missingness, and uncertainty tests should be published?

## Proposed reviewers and roles

### Case Western Reserve University

Use the founder’s alumni connection for an introductory conversation with
nonprofit management, social policy, evaluation, or philanthropy faculty.
Case’s nonprofit leadership and research history make it a useful independent
methods conversation, especially for nonprofit governance and sector context.

### Rice University

The Center for Philanthropy & Nonprofit Leadership is a Houston-based fit for
philanthropy, nonprofit leadership, and direct community engagement.

### University of Houston-Downtown

The nonprofit management program is a useful practitioner-connected review
lane, particularly for operational burden and the realities of smaller local
organizations.

### Community reviewers

- Two or more smaller Houston nonprofit leaders
- One fundraising or development professional
- One donor or donor-advisor perspective
- One statistician or fairness researcher familiar with incomplete data

## Review package

- Current methodology and data dictionary
- Data provenance and freshness rules
- Coverage and missingness report
- Examples of scored, unscored, and borderline organizations
- Synthetic edge cases
- Proposed donor-facing language
- Privacy and nonprofit-provided-information boundaries
- Specific questions for critique

## Desired outputs

- Written methods critique
- Missingness and coverage risk assessment
- Fairness and sensitivity test recommendations
- Donor comprehension and language feedback
- Clear list of claims Daanaa should not make
- Decision record showing what changed, what did not, and why

## Outreach posture

Ask for a short exploratory conversation first. Do not ask for a public
endorsement, logo use, formal partnership, data transfer, or student labor
before the scope and review safeguards are agreed.

## Current decision gate

Do not expand financial-context claims for data-dark nonprofits until this
review is complete or the limitation is explicitly accepted and documented by
the founder.
