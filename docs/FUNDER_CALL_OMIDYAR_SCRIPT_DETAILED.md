## The Call

---

### The Problem (2 min)

> "Here's the systems problem we're addressing: Technology is increasingly shaping how resources flow in the nonprofit sector. Algorithms decide visibility. Data shapes funding. But most of these systems are proprietary, opaque, and optimized for engagement—not fairness.
>
> Meanwhile, smaller nonprofits—often the most innovative and community-rooted—lose out because they don't have the digital sophistication or brand to game the algorithms.
>
> **This is a power imbalance that technology is making worse, not better.**
>
> Daanaa is proof that technology can do the opposite: make systems more transparent, more fair, and more participatory."

---

### Our Solution (3 min)

> "We're building infrastructure for nonprofit discovery using a simple principle: **Trust signals must be evidence-based, governance must be transparent, and AI must be a tool for fairness, not concentration.**
>
> Here's how:
>
> **1. No black boxes:** Scores derive from public IRS data. Any donor can see the methodology. No proprietary algorithm deciding who's 'worthy.'
>
> **2. Participatory governance:** We use a Stewardship Commitment—a public contract binding us to 11 principles. Donors, nonprofits, and partners can audit us. If we drift, anyone can call it out.
>
> **3. AI as a fairness tool:** We use AI for mission tagging and embeddings—tools that augment human judgment, not replace it. All AI outputs are batch-reviewed before surfacing. No autonomous decisions on visibility or ranking.
>
> **4. Structural independence:** No vendor can pay to change their visibility. No funder can influence org rankings. Independence is built in, not aspirational.
>
> The result: A system that small nonprofits trust. Where power is distributed, not concentrated. Where technology makes systems more fair, not less."

## Likely Omidyar Questions

**"How do you actually prevent vendor/funder influence if someone tries hard?"**

> "Structurally, not contractually. Here's how:
>
> - Scores compute from public data, algorithmically, with zero human curation per org
> - Visibility ranking doesn't exist—we show search results, not 'best orgs'
> - No vendor can pay to boost presence
> - Any vendor attempting influence triggers removal + public disclosure (part of Stewardship Commitment)
>
> **But here's the real safeguard:** You and other partners sign the Stewardship Commitment. You can audit us. If we ever build secret ranking logic or hidden vendor influence, you can call it out publicly.
>
> This is participatory governance in practice. You have structural oversight."

**"What happens in year 2+ when you need revenue?"**

> "Good question. We're exploring: premium services for nonprofits (compliance letters, visibility tools), not-for-profit transaction infrastructure, licensed research data.
>
> **But none of these touch the discovery layer.** The core—free, fair, evidence-based—stays sacrosanct.
>
> How do we protect this? The Stewardship Commitment. Any revenue model that threatens core principles is a breach. You can veto."

**"How do you govern AI decisions as you scale?"**

> "Phase 1 (Aug 15): No autonomous AI decisions on visibility or ranking. Agents handle operations (email, onboarding validation) with human approval gates.
>
> Phase 2+ (Oct+): As we add features (volunteer matching, board recruitment), AI helps with matching logic. But all matching is opt-in, all results are explainable, all outcomes are auditable.
>
> **Every autonomous AI decision gets documented in our governance log.** Partners can review quarterly. If a decision violates principles, it's reverted.
>
> This is what responsible AI governance looks like."

**"How do you measure success beyond growth metrics?"**

> "Three categories:
>
> **Fairness metrics:** Do small orgs receive proportional donor interest? Is the hidden gems algorithm surfacing underfunded organizations?
>
> **Governance metrics:** How many times did we face pressure to compromise principles? Did we hold? Are audit processes working?
>
> **Trust metrics:** Do nonprofits and donors trust the platform? Do they believe our independence is real?
>
> We'll publish these in annual impact reports—not just MAU and funding."

**"What's your stance on data privacy for nonprofits themselves?"**

> "Structural. We don't profile nonprofits beyond what they file publicly or claim themselves. We don't sell nonprofit data. We don't build behavioral profiles.
>
> Nonprofits own their own data. If they claim a profile, they can edit/delete it anytime. We don't build dependency."
