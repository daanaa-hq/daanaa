## The Problem (Schmidt Angle)

### AI Governance in Institutional Decision-Making

AI is entering domains where it shouldn't operate autonomously: nonprofit evaluation, resource allocation, institutional trust.

**The gap:** Without transparent, auditable AI governance models, the sector will default to:
- Proprietary black-box scoring systems
- AI trained on biased historical data (replicating past inequities)
- Optimization for funder goals (not nonprofit quality or mission alignment)
- No appeal or correction mechanism

**Where Daanaa fits:** We demonstrate that trustworthy, transparent AI can serve civic infrastructure **without** surveillance, manipulation, or extractive data practices.

---

## Our Approach: AI Governance in Practice

**Daanaa uses AI responsibly in three ways:**

### 1. Mission Generation (Transparent, Reviewable)
- Local AI (Qwen2.5-32B) generates org descriptions from IRS data
- **Reviewed before public** (not auto-published)
- **Fails openly** (missing data shown as missing, not inferred)
- **Not used for ranking** (only context)
- **Auditable pipeline** (can inspect inputs + outputs)

### 2. Financial Benchmarking (Deterministic, Not ML)
- Scores from IRS data using published formulas
- **No black-box ML models** (all methodology public)
- **Fair peer groups** (NTEE code + revenue band, not algorithmic)
- **Reproducible** (anyone can verify using public data)
- **Appeals process** (nonprofit can correct or request re-eval)

### 3. Semantic Search (Local, Transparent)
- Embeddings via local LLM (not cloud API)
- **User controls the query** (they decide intent)
- **Ranking is relevance-based** (not quality judgment)
- **No personalization** (no manipulation, no engagement optimization)

### What We Don't Do
- ❌ Extract nonprofit data for AI training
- ❌ Use AI to make autonomous decisions
- ❌ Optimize algorithms for funder influence
- ❌ Create proprietary scoring black boxes
- ❌ Expose individual nonprofits to comparison pressure

## The Governance Framework

**Daanaa operates under 11 founding principles:**
- Independence protected (no funder influence on outcomes)
- Trust signals evidence-based (only public data)
- AI as tool, not replacement (humans decide)
- Principles gate all decisions (audit before ship)
- Mistakes corrected openly (public registry)

**Every decision—human and AI—is auditable against these principles.**

---

## Why This Matters for AI Governance

**The model problem:** Most AI in civic tech optimizes for engagement, influence, and funder goals. Daanaa optimizes for **transparency, nonprofit agency, and donor autonomy**.

**The governance problem:** Most systems have no appeal or correction mechanism. Daanaa has a public Mistake Registry + re-evaluation process.

**The sustainability problem:** Most AI systems are cloud-dependent and proprietary. Daanaa uses local compute + open methodology (no vendor lock-in, no opaque dependencies).
