## Talking Points (In conversation order)

### Opening (2 min)
"I'm building Daanaa, a free nonprofit-discovery platform that uses Claude AI (and local inference) to help donors understand organizational context. We're demonstrating responsible AI at scale — explainable methodology, local-first infrastructure, structural fairness.

We're launching publicly in August and looking for founding partners. We think Omidyar would be interested because we're literally building the kind of responsible AI governance you fund — in production, serving real users."

### Why This Is AI Governance (2 min)
"Daanaa is a case study in responsible AI: we use Claude for analysis, but humans make all final decisions. Scoring is deterministic from public data, not black-box neural networks. Every score is explainable. We run inference locally on our own hardware for privacy and auditability.

This is the kind of AI governance question Omidyar cares about: how do you deploy AI in a way that's fair, transparent, and deserving of public trust?"

### The Ask (1 min)
"We're looking for 3–5 founding partners. $400K over 18 months. Partners get: you're funding a production case study in responsible AI, advisory role (input on our methodology, governance, transparency), public credibility (founding partner status), and a potential research partnership.

In exchange, you sign a Stewardship Commitment protecting our independence, fairness, and privacy."

### Why This Matters for Omidyar (2 min)
"Right now, the conversation around AI governance is mostly theoretical. We're building something that proves responsible AI can scale. By being a founding partner, you're not just funding a tool — you're funding evidence that your thesis works: AI *can* be deployed responsibly at scale, in public-good contexts, with structural accountability."

### Pause for questions / objections

**Common Omidyar question:** "How do you ensure the AI isn't biasing results?"
**Answer:** "Scoring is deterministic from IRS data, not learned from patterns. AI is used for embeddings (semantic search) and mission tagging — outputs are batch-reviewed before surfacing. We sandbox-test with 50 nonprofits before public launch to catch any unintended bias. Local inference means we audit everything."

**Common Omidyar question:** "What about fairness to small orgs or underrepresented communities?"
**Answer:** "Structural fairness is a core principle. Scores are benchmarked within NTEE peer groups (a $200K nonprofit is never compared to Kaiser). Hidden gems mechanic explicitly surfaces small, overlooked, high-performing orgs. We actively monitor for geographic, scale, and demographic bias."

**Common Omidyar question:** "How do you prevent this from becoming another extractive data platform?"
**Answer:** "Data is local-first. We don't build user profiles, don't sell insights, don't track donors. The Giving Wallet is device-local and account-free. Nonprofits can't buy higher visibility. It's structurally non-extractive."

**Common Omidyar question:** "Why not seek traditional AI governance funding or academic partnerships?"
**Answer:** "We do want academic partnerships — that's exactly the kind of rigor we need. But we're also production infrastructure that has to work in the real world. We're looking for partners who value both: rigorous governance *and* real deployment. You do both."

## How to Handle Hesitation

**If they say:** "This is interesting, but we usually fund larger governance initiatives"
**You say:** "Fair. But I'd argue this is exactly the scale where responsible AI deployment gets proven or disproven. We're trying to show it works in a real, public-good context. That data would be valuable for everything else Omidyar funds."

**If they say:** "We'd want to see your methodology reviewed by third parties"
**You say:** "Absolutely. We're planning to publish our full methodology before public launch and welcome independent review. Would you want to be part of that process?"

**If they say:** "What about liability if something goes wrong?"
**Answer:** "Good question. We have structural safeguards: human-in-command, explainability, local auditing, privacy-first design. We're also discussing indemnification with our attorney as part of the Stewardship Commitment. I can share that framework once it's locked in."

## Red Flags (When NOT to pursue)

- "We'd want to influence the methodology for our grantees' benefit" → **Not a fit.** (Stewardship Principle 7: Independence.)
- "We'd want access to nonprofit data for our own research" → **Not a fit.** (Stewardship Principle 2: Privacy.)
- "This is too early-stage for us to invest in" → **Okay,** but you may need to come back after sandbox results.
