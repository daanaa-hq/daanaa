THE CHALLENGE
Compliance breaks when technology changes
Most AI governance is written as rules: "Don't do X." Rules describe machines of the moment.
When the technology changes, the rules either become ignored or get violated in spirit while obeyed in letter.
What we need: governance built on purpose, not just prohibition.

THE SHIFT
Stewardship beats rules
Stewardship asks: "What was I built for? Does this serve that purpose?"
Rules ask: "What does the rulebook forbid?"
Stewardship scales with capability. Rules don't—more capable systems following rules blindly become more dangerous, not safer.

OBLIGATIONS 1-2
Mission & Oversight
1. Mission Alignment
Every capability must be traceable to the mission. If you can't answer "how does this serve our purpose?" you don't build it.
2. Human Oversight
A machine may draft; a human answers for what is done. Every AI output touching public records needs human verification first.

OBLIGATIONS 3-5
Transparency & Correction
3. Explainability
If you can't explain it, you can't publish it. Every decision gets logged with its reasoning.
4. Information Provenance
Every fact carries its origin. AI-discovered data stays labeled until a human verifies it.
5. Correction
When wrong, we fix it, document it, and prevent the class of error.

OBLIGATIONS 6-9
Appeals, Respect & Ethics
6. Appeals
Anyone affected by an AI judgment can appeal to a human with authority to overturn it.
9. Ethics Before Optimization
When efficiency conflicts with principles, forfeit the efficiency. No accuracy gain justifies exposing privacy or ranking worth.

KEY TRUTH
Responsibility doesn't scale
More capable systems do not equal more autonomous systems.
Responsibility stays human, at every level of machine intelligence, forever. That's not a limitation we tolerate—that's the design.
