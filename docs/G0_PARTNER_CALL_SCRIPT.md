# Partner Call Script — Founding Stewardship Signatory Ask

**Duration:** 20–30 minutes  
**Goal:** Gauge interest in becoming a Founding Stewardship Signatory (not a formal pitch, exploratory conversation)

---

## Pre-Call Prep (For You)

✓ Have STEWARDSHIP.md open (you'll reference it)  
✓ Know their foundation's mission/recent grants  
✓ Have budget range ready ($100–250K over 18 months)  
✓ Take notes on their reactions and questions

---

## The Call

### **Opening (2 min)**

**You say:**
> "Hi [Name], thanks for making time. I wanted to call before we go through any formal application process. We're building something different in the giving space — infrastructure that helps everyone rise together.
>
> Do you have 20 minutes?"

*[Wait for yes]*

---

### **Context (3 min)**

**You say:**
> "Daanaa is infrastructure for how nonprofits succeed. We're building a complete ecosystem where three types of support flow to mission-driven organizations: donor capital, volunteer talent, and board leadership.
>
> Here's the problem we're solving: Small nonprofits are invisible. Not because they lack impact, but because donors default to size + brand. There's no peer context, no volunteer matching, no board recruitment infrastructure. They're competing blind.
>
> We're fixing that — with evidence-based financial context, direct volunteer-to-nonprofit matching, and board recruitment. All transparent, all privacy-first, all fair.
>
> We've built the full infrastructure (scoring, claiming, volunteer signals, board matching) and we're launching publicly in August. We're seeking 3–5 founding partners who not only fund us, but who believe in this vision: everyone rises together.
>
> Your work on [their mission area] tells me you believe in fairness and mission. I wanted to explore: Would you be interested in being a Founding Stewardship Signatory?"

*[Let them respond. Don't push yet.]*

---

### **What That Means (5 min)**

**They might ask:** "What does that entail?"

**You say:**
> "Great question. A Founding Stewardship Signatory means:
>
> **From us:** $100–250K in funding over 18 months, advisor role on quarterly calls, and public recognition as a founding partner.
>
> **From you:** You'd sign our Stewardship Commitment — which says you endorse infrastructure that's evidence-based, respects privacy, treats small orgs fairly, and connects them with the three types of support they need: donor capital, volunteer talent, board leadership.
>
> **What signing does NOT mean:** You're not controlling operations, you're not liable for our actions, you're not getting equity. You're endorsing principles and advising strategically.
>
> **The sequencing:** We launch with donor search + nonprofit claiming (Aug). Then volunteer matching (Sep). Then board recruitment (Oct). Then sustainable services (year 2). Each phase builds on the last. Everyone rises together.
>
> **Why partnership matters:** We're not building a commercial platform. We're building public infrastructure that small nonprofits deserve. That requires partners who believe in fairness, not growth hacking.
>
> Does that resonate?"

*[Listen. This is the key moment.]*

---

### **If They're Intrigued (Next Steps)**

**You say:**
> "I'm really glad you see the value. I want to be transparent: this is exploratory. I'm talking to 4–5 potential partners to see who's genuinely aligned.
>
> If you're interested, here's what I'd suggest:
>
> 1. **I send you our Stewardship Commitment** (full document) + a Partner Agreement summary
> 2. **You review internally** with your team/board (no pressure, no timeline)
> 3. **We schedule a second call** in 2 weeks where you ask all the hard questions
> 4. **If it feels right, we talk budgets and timeline**
>
> The goal is to move fast but not recklessly. We want partners who genuinely believe in this, not partners who are checking a box.
>
> Does that work?"

*[If yes: Take their email, confirm timing, offer to schedule the second call now.]*

---

### **If They Have Questions on the Call**

**They might ask:**

**"Why not apply for our normal grants?"**
> "We could, but I wanted to see if you'd be interested in something deeper — not just funding, but partnership. We think having 3–5 founding partners who actually believe in our principles matters more than applying to 15 funders generically."

**"What's your timeline?"**
> "We're aiming for public launch in July/August. We'd want to have founding partners locked in by then so we can announce together. But if you need more time, we can work with that."

**"What's the ask amount?"**
> "$100–250K over 18 months, depending on what makes sense for you. We're flexible. More important than the number is that you believe in the model."

**"What about our existing grantees?"**
> "Good question. Signing our Stewardship Commitment doesn't restrict your other grantmaking. It just means you're endorsing how we build giving infrastructure. You can fund other platforms too."

**"How do you make money?"**
> "EcoMargins (our parent company) funds Daanaa as a free public good. In the future, we might offer premium services (letters, visibility tools) to nonprofits, but the core platform will stay free."

**"Why do you need partners to sign your principles?"**
> "Because we've learned (from the sector) that independence and principles are fragile. Signing the commitment is a way of saying 'we're all in on this' — it's not just words. And if we ever drift from our principles, you can call us out publicly."

---

### **Closing (2 min)**

**If they're interested:**
> "I'm excited about this possibility. Let me send you everything tonight — Stewardship Commitment, Partner Agreement summary, and a calendar link for our second call. No pressure, just take a look and let me know what questions come up."

**If they need to think:**
> "I appreciate you taking the time. Totally understand this is a new model. Take your time reviewing, and let me know if you want to explore further."

**If they're not interested:**
> "I respect that. If things shift on your end, I'd love to revisit. And either way, I appreciate your work on [mission area]."

---

## Key Things to Remember

- **This is exploratory.** You're not pitching a grant. You're exploring partnership.
- **Listen more than you talk.** Their questions reveal what they care about.
- **Be honest about the stage.** You're pre-launch, still developing. That's OK.
- **Don't oversell.** The principles should sell themselves to the right funders.
- **Take notes.** Their feedback helps you refine the ask for the next call.

---

## Red Flags (If You Hear These, They Might Not Be the Right Partner)

- "We fund tech, not nonprofits" → Not aligned
- "We want equity or control" → Incompatible model
- "Our donors want ROI metrics" → Different value system
- "We need to see growth metrics before funding" → Transactional mindset
- "Can you adjust principles based on what we fund?" → They're trying to control you

**Green Flags (Signs They're the Right Partner):**

- "This is what we've been looking for"
- "How do we help you protect these principles?"
- "We want to amplify this model"
- "This is bigger than a single grant"
- "Can we help with other partners?"

---

**Good luck. You're not asking for money. You're recruiting believers.**
