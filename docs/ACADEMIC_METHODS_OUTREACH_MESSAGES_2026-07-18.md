## Case Western Reserve University

### Subject

Case alum seeking independent critique of a nonprofit financial context methodology

### Message

Hello,

My name is Akbar Khowaja, and I am a Case Western Reserve University alum based in Houston. I am building Daanaa, an independent nonprofit discovery platform intended to help donors find mission-driven organizations, including smaller organizations that may have meaningful work but limited public visibility.

Daanaa currently uses public IRS and related nonprofit records to show peer financial context. The current method looks at operating reserves relative to organizations with similar funding models and revenue size. It is designed as context rather than a rating, judgment, or measure of mission impact.

I am looking for a careful academic critique of the method, especially its treatment of nonprofits whose public financial data is missing, partial, delayed, or structurally unavailable. My concern is that data availability can reflect organizational size and administrative capacity. A system meant to improve discovery could unintentionally favor organizations that are already better documented.

I am not asking Case Western Reserve to endorse Daanaa. I would value a short exploratory conversation about whether someone in nonprofit management, social policy, evaluation, philanthropy, or related research might be willing to review the methodology and identify risks we should address.

The most relevant pages are:

- [About Daanaa](https://daanaa.org/about)
- [Methodology](https://daanaa.org/methodology)
- [Stewardship Charter](https://daanaa.org/charter)
- [Governance](https://daanaa.org/governance)

The questions I most want challenged are:

- When should Daanaa decline to calculate a financial context signal?
- How should missingness be measured across organization size, cause, geography, and age?
- What language keeps “no public evidence” separate from “financial concern”?
- What fairness and sensitivity tests should be required before expanding the system?

If this sounds appropriate for an initial conversation, I would be grateful for a referral to the right faculty member, center, or practitioner connected with the university.
