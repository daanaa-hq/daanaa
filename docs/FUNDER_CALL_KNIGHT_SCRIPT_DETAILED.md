## The Call

### Opening (1.5 min)

> "Hi [Name], thanks for taking the call. I'm building infrastructure for nonprofit discovery, and your work on civic engagement got my attention. I think there's a strategic connection here. Do you have 20 minutes to explore it?"

---

### The Problem (2 min)

> "Here's the civic problem: Americans want to give locally and meaningfully. But most donors don't know where to give. They Google 'nonprofits near me' and get either huge national organizations or a confusing local directory with no context.
>
> Meanwhile, thousands of local nonprofits—solving real community problems—go unfunded because they're invisible.
>
> **This breaks informed citizenship.** You can't make good giving decisions if you don't have good information. You can't be a good civic participant if you don't understand your community's needs.
>
> Daanaa fixes this."

---

### Our Solution (3 min)

> "We're building a directory of 1.87 million nonprofits with three types of information:
>
> **1. Evidence:** Financial context from public IRS data. How is this org doing relative to mission-matched peers? Where are they strongest?
>
> **2. Local focus:** Search by location, cause, and financial health. Find the nonprofits solving problems in your community right now.
>
> **3. Hidden gems:** Algorithmic surfacing of small, high-impact, overlooked organizations. Not because we think they're 'better'—but because they wouldn't appear in a normal search, and they deserve to be seen.
>
> The result: A donor can ask, 'What education nonprofits are doing well in my city?' and get transparent, data-driven answers. No algorithms deciding who 'wins.' Just evidence.
>
> **This is informed citizenship for giving.** And it's free, open, and governed by principles—not profit."

---

### Why Knight (2 min)

> "Knight has invested in informed citizenship for decades. Informed voters. Informed media consumers. But informed donors? That infrastructure doesn't exist yet.
>
> Daanaa is how you scale informed giving. Not through programs. Through durable, transparent infrastructure that any donor can trust."

## Likely Knight Questions

**"How does this work for really small local nonprofits—ones that barely file 990s?"**

> "Great question. We use IRS public data as the source of truth. Small orgs file too—even if simplified. We also allow nonprofits to claim and edit their own profiles, add mission statements, and surface their work.
>
> So a small local org can be in our directory two ways: through IRS data + public research, OR by claiming their profile and filling it in themselves.
>
> This is fair (P4 - small orgs get equal treatment) and inclusive."

**"Won't this just promote donors giving nationally instead of locally?"**

> "We actually do the opposite. You can search by location—'education nonprofits in Austin, TX.' We also highlight local hidden gems: small, healthy, overlooked orgs in your community.
>
> Our goal is not to maximize giving to big national orgs. It's to maximize informed giving wherever donors want to give."

**"How do you measure impact on civic participation?"**

> "Three metrics:
> 1. **Visibility:** How many more donors discover nonprofits they wouldn't have found through Google?
> 2. **Trust:** Do donors feel more confident in their giving decisions?
> 3. **Equity:** Do small/local/overlooked orgs see proportional donor interest?
>
> We'll share these publicly in annual reports. You'll see the data."

**"What about donors who want to understand impact beyond financials?"**

> "That's a future phase. Year 2+, we'll integrate nonprofit-provided outcome data, volunteer impact metrics, peer reviews. But launch is about financial context and discovery—the foundation.
>
> We're intentional about phasing. Do one thing well, then add complexity."

**"How does this relate to local news/local journalism?"**

> "Interesting angle. Local news should be covering local nonprofits. Right now, they often lack the data to tell good stories. Daanaa surfaces that data—'Here's a local nonprofit doing excellent work.'
>
> Long term, we see partnerships: Daanaa surfaces stories, local news covers them, more donors learn about local work. It's complementary."
