# Article Strategy: Thoughtful Data Journalism

**Voice:** Human, conversational, intellectually honest  
**Approach:** Data-backed insights + sector/regional analysis + predictability with caveats  
**Format:** 2,000-4,000 words, cited, downloadable  
**Audience:** Nonprofit leaders, policymakers, researchers, donors who want to understand the sector

### Series 2: "Predictability & Limits: What Public Data Can Tell Us"

**Main article:** What we can predict about nonprofit health, and what we can't

**Thesis:**
> Public nonprofit data is powerful but incomplete. We can predict *trajectory* with reasonable confidence (will this org be healthier next year?). We cannot predict *mission quality* (is this org actually helping people?) or *individual decisions* (will this nonprofit close?). Understanding the difference matters.

**What we CAN predict (with evidence):**
- Reserve trends (based on 3-year operating margin history)
- Sustainability by funding model (fee-for-service vs. donation-funded)
- Sector viability (which causes are growing, which are contracting)
- Geographic factors (urban vs. rural nonprofit density)

**What we CANNOT predict (and why):**
- Mission impact (IRS data doesn't measure outcome)
- Leadership quality (no data on who's in charge)
- Individual org closure (causation ≠ correlation)
- Donor behavior (we don't track individual giving)
- Strategic pivots (sudden decisions don't show in financials until year 2)

**Sub-article: "The Limits of Transparency"**
- Honest reckoning: what our data doesn't capture
- Why mission matters more than metrics
- The danger of optimizing for measurability
- What policymakers get wrong about nonprofit "health"

### Series 3: "AI Governance & Empathy in Nonprofit Data"

**Main article:** How Daanaa uses AI responsibly, and why nonprofits should care

**Thesis:**
> AI can amplify bias in nonprofit data—labeling small orgs as "risky," hiding low-profile organizations from view, or automating decisions that should be human. We're building guardrails. Here's why it matters.

**Key sections:**
1. **The Problem:** How data systems marginalize small nonprofits
   - "CAUTION" labels discourage giving to lean organizations
   - Algorithms favor large, well-documented orgs
   - Automation can hide the most interesting work

2. **Our Approach:** Stewardship commitments that protect organizations
   - No automated org ranking above human judgment
   - Language designed for empathy, not shame
   - Small org fairness built into scoring
   - Data governance that respects nonprofit independence

3. **Questions for Other Platforms**
   - Who decided this algorithm is fair?
   - Who benefits when organizations are ranked by size?
   - What happens to orgs that fall below the algorithm's threshold?
   - Can you opt out of algorithmic visibility?

4. **A Call for Better AI Governance**
   - Public funding should demand public accountability
   - Nonprofit data systems need nonprofit input
   - Transparency isn't transparency if you can't understand it

## Format & Structure

### Article Template

```
# [Title]: [Subtitle That Answers a Real Question]

## The Question
What's actually happening in [topic]? Why should you care?

## The Data
[2-3 key statistics with sources]

## What It Means
[Interpretation—honest about uncertainty]

## Where It's Working
[Example sectors/regions/orgs doing well]

## Where It's Broken
[Gaps, challenges, who's left out]

## What We Don't Know
[Caveats, limitations, what IRS data can't measure]

## What Happens Next
[Trends, predictions with confidence levels]

## How You Can Help
[Action: read more, share data request, get involved]

---

*Data sources: IRS Form 990 (ProPublica), IRS SOI (2023-2025 filings), Daanaa analysis of 1.7M organizations. Published [date]. Last updated [date].*
```

### Citation Standards

Every claim gets a source:
- **IRS data:** "According to IRS Form 990 data for 2024 filings (N=1.7M orgs), ..."
- **Our analysis:** "Our analysis of peer-grouped financial health across 537K orgs with complete financial data shows ..."
- **Sector research:** "A 2025 study by [Org] found that ... (see: [link])"
- **Confidence levels:** "We can say with ~80% confidence that ... because ..."

### Honest Uncertainty

Use language that's specific about what we know:
- "About 1 in 5 food banks report less than 3 months of reserves—but we don't know if that's by design or necessity." ✓
- "Food banks are in crisis." ✗ (too definitive, implies blame)

2. **Fact-check:** Verify every statistic, source, confidence level
3. **Founder review:** Tone, messaging, strategic framing

## Why This Works

1. **Authenticity:** Honest about limits builds trust more than claims of omniscience
2. **Shareability:** Human writing gets shared; academic writing gets cited
3. **SEO:** Sector + regional keywords we rank for naturally
4. **Positioning:** Shows Daanaa as thoughtful custodian of nonprofit data, not a judge
5. **Advocacy:** Raises real questions about AI governance in the sector
6. **Impact:** Influencers, policymakers, researchers cite our work → Daanaa becomes the reference
