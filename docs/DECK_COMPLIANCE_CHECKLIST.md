# "Why Giving Matters" Deck — Stewardship Compliance Checklist

## Pre-Build Audit Against Stewardship Commitment

### Principle 1: Mission Before Growth
- [ ] Deck exists to **educate donors**, not drive traffic
- [ ] No hidden agenda to funnel people to Daanaa
- [ ] Daanaa positioned as optional support, not the payoff
- [ ] Every claim serves "informed giving," not "Daanaa growth"
- **Rule:** If removing Daanaa entirely still makes the deck valuable → passes

### Principle 2: Privacy is Core
- [ ] No tracking/surveillance language
- [ ] No encouragement of public giving/social performance
- [ ] No suggestion that giving must be shared or verified publicly
- [ ] Framing: Giving is personal, private, sincere
- **Rule:** Deck respects the wallet philosophy (local, private, optional account)

### Principle 3: Evidence-Based, Honestly Stated
- [ ] Every fact cited with source
- [ ] No overclaiming from studies (if 70% happy, don't say "overwhelming evidence")
- [ ] Uncertainties acknowledged (if research is mixed, say so)
- [ ] No "everyone knows" claims without data
- [ ] Methodology transparent (which studies, when, sample size if relevant)
- **Rule:** A researcher could verify every claim in 10 minutes

### Principle 4: Small Org Fairness
- [ ] Don't bias history toward famous/large givers
- [ ] Don't suggest small giving is less important
- [ ] Honor mutual aid / grassroots traditions equally to formal philanthropy
- [ ] Acknowledge that giving capacity varies, value does not
- **Rule:** A $10 monthly giver is framed with same dignity as a $1M donor

### Principle 5: Don't Weaponize Transparency
- [ ] No shame framing ("nonprofits are broken," "donors are selfish")
- [ ] No outrage language (don't optimize for anger/guilt)
- [ ] No "F-rated" framing of giving problems
- [ ] Tone: Curious, investigative, not accusatory
- **Rule:** Avoid language designed to make people feel bad; instead show them why it matters

### Principle 6: Mistakes Corrected Quickly
- [ ] If research contradicts itself, acknowledge it
- [ ] If data is outdated, note the year
- [ ] If a source turns out to be wrong, plan to fix it
- [ ] Build in mechanism to update slides if facts change
- **Rule:** Corrections are visible, not hidden

### Principle 7: Independence Protected
- [ ] No nonprofit or partner can have influenced which research we cite
- [ ] Sources are primary (research institutions, not advocacy orgs with an agenda)
- [ ] No hidden sponsorships or partnerships
- [ ] Daanaa's role doesn't create conflicts (we're not asking nonprofits to promote us)
- **Rule:** Deck would work even if Daanaa didn't exist

### Principle 8: Never Control Funds
- [ ] Don't suggest Daanaa processes donations (we don't)
- [ ] Don't create pressure to give through Daanaa
- [ ] Hand-off framing: "Here's where to give" not "Give here"
- [ ] Wallet is mentioned as *optional* bookmarking tool
- **Rule:** Giving decision stays with the donor, hand-off to nonprofit

### Principle 9: Decisions Explainable
- [ ] Why we chose these specific studies
- [ ] Why we framed history this way
- [ ] Why Daanaa is positioned at the end
- [ ] Why we use these charts/visualizations
- **Rule:** Document the reasoning; audit trail is clear

### Principle 10: Human in Command
- [ ] You review every slide before publication
- [ ] I don't publish autonomously
- [ ] You have final say on messaging
- [ ] Accountability stays with you
- **Rule:** I build, you decide

### Principle 11: Principles Not Weakened
- [ ] Deck doesn't erode these principles to gain scale/traffic
- [ ] If choice between purity and reach, we choose purity
- [ ] No compromises for "efficiency"
- **Rule:** "We could reach more people if we..." is not a valid reason to bend principles

## Content Red Flags (Will Reject)

🚫 **Do not include if:**
- Any claim without a cited source
- Anecdotes without context ("one donor changed a life" without data)
- Shame language ("nonprofits are broken," "donors don't care")
- Pressure tactics (urgency framing, scarcity, FOMO)
- Celebrity giving unless it makes a data point
- Daanaa framed as the solution (it's optional support)
- Unverified studies or cherry-picked research
- Oversimplification ("generosity solves poverty" — no, it's part of the picture)
- Bias toward wealthy givers or large donations
- Any suggestion that giving requires public sharing

---

## Research Standards (Minimum)

**For every fact in the deck:**
1. Primary source identified (researcher's name, institution, year)
2. Sample size/methodology disclosed (if relevant)
3. Limitations acknowledged (if study is small or narrow)
4. Publication year noted (so reader knows age of data)
5. Link or reference provided (reader can verify)

**For Daanaa data:**
- Registry numbers verified against actual database
- No rounding that misrepresents scale
- Methodology transparent (what counts as a "nonprofit"?)
- Data freshness noted

---

## Tone Guardrails

✅ **Do use:**
- "Research shows..."
- "Studies suggest..."
- "Data indicates..."
- "The history of giving reveals..."
- "Generosity appears to..."
- Curious, investigative voice
- Question-driven ("Why do humans give?")
- Humble language ("We don't fully understand...")

❌ **Don't use:**
- "Proven that..."
- "Everyone knows..."
- "Obviously..."
- "The science is settled..."
- Prescriptive language ("You should...")
- Pressure/urgency ("Act now...")
- Shame language ("Donors ignore...")

## If There's Ever a Conflict

**Principle wins over engagement.**

Example:
- Research might say "Fear of shame makes people give" → We won't use shame tactics even if effective
- Data might show large orgs get more traffic → We won't bias toward them
- Daanaa could drive more signups if pushy → We stay optional

**The deck serves Daanaa's mission (informed giving), not Daanaa's growth.**
