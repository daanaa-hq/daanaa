# Attorney Call Script — Jun 16, 9am

**Duration:** 30 minutes (book 45 min to be safe)

**Goal:** Lock in legal structure for accepting grants, confirm Stewardship Commitment signing is defensible, understand public signatory list implications.

---

## Opening (2 min)

"Hi [Name], thanks for taking the call. I'm building Daanaa, a free nonprofit-discovery platform (daanaa.org) that uses public IRS data to help donors make more informed giving decisions. The parent company is EcoMargins Consulting LLC, which I formed in December 2025. We're now seeking funding to accelerate the work.

I have three specific legal questions that will shape our fundraising and partnership strategy. I've got about 30 minutes — does that work?"

---

## Question 1: Entity Structure for Grants (8 min)

**What you're asking:**
"Here's the situation: Daanaa is a DBA under EcoMargins LLC, which is a for-profit entity. We're looking to raise $400K over 18 months from foundations and mission-aligned funders. The funding would support:
- AI infrastructure and operations ($50–80K/year)
- 1 contract engineer (Jul–Sep, fulltime, ~$30K)
- Research and compliance work ($60–80K/year)
- Operations and QA systems

**Question 1a:** Can EcoMargins LLC accept grant funding directly from foundations and nonprofit funders? Or do we need a fiscal sponsor structure?

**Question 1b:** If we do need a fiscal sponsor, what are the implications? Cost, timeline, tax structure?

**Question 1c:** If EcoMargins can accept grants directly, are there any compliance or tax implications we should know about?"

---

**Why you're asking:** You need to know if your current structure blocks funding or if you need to add a layer.

**Listen for:**
- "Yes, for-profits can accept grants, but..." (cost, limitations, conditions)
- "You should form a nonprofit" (major pivot; ask why)
- "Fiscal sponsor is the cleanest path" (follow-up: cost + timeline)
- "It's complicated, depends on the funder" (good sign — means there's flexibility)

**Follow-up if needed:**
- "So if DRK Foundation wants to fund this, they can give directly to EcoMargins, correct?"
- "What paperwork do we need to provide them?"

---

## Question 2: Stewardship Commitment Signing (5 min)

**What you're asking:**
"We're structuring partnerships differently than traditional grants. We're asking founding partners to sign a 'Stewardship Commitment' — an 11-point agreement that covers things like:
- No paid placement or algorithmic favoritism in our scores
- Donor privacy protections (we don't give partner access to giving data)
- Independence guarantees (partner funds our work but doesn't influence our rankings)
- Regular public reporting on how we're adhering to these principles

**Question 2:** From a legal perspective, is there any risk to:
- Us asking partners to sign this commitment?
- Partners signing something that binds them to our principles?
- Publishing the list of who's signed?"

---

**Why you're asking:** You want to know if Stewardship is legally defensible or if it exposes you or your partners to liability.

**Listen for:**
- "This is fine; it's a memorandum of understanding, not a binding contract" (ideal)
- "You should have an attorney review the language" (ask if they can do it or recommend someone)
- "This could be seen as a condition of funding" (follow-up: is that a problem?)
- "Be careful about public signatory lists" (follow-up: what are the risks?)

**Follow-up if needed:**
- "Is there a simple legal structure we should use for the commitment document itself?"
- "If a partner changes leadership, do they need to re-sign?"
- "Can we publish partner names publicly or should we keep it confidential?"

---

## Question 3: Public Signatory List (5 min)

**What you're asking:**
"We want to publish the list of founding partners publicly — on our website, in press materials, etc. It's part of our credibility signal: 'Daanaa is backed by [DRK Foundation, Knight Foundation, Omidyar Network].'

**Question 3:** Any legal concerns with publishing funder names publicly?"

---

**Why you're asking:** This is a credibility play for you and the partners. You need to know if it's safe.

**Listen for:**
- "No, this is normal" (ideal)
- "Check the partner's communications policy" (ask how you'd do this)
- "Watch for conflicts of interest disclosures" (follow-up: examples?)

**Follow-up if needed:**
- "Can we use their logos on our site?"
- "Do we need their permission for each mention, or is one-time agreement enough?"

## If Attorney Says "You Need a Nonprofit"

Don't panic. Ask:

1. "What's the timeline to form one?"
2. "Can EcoMargins continue operating while we set up a nonprofit?"
3. "Would the nonprofit be a subsidiary of EcoMargins, or separate?"
4. "Roughly what's the cost and process?"

Then: **Stop and update Akbar immediately.** This is a significant pivot, but it's manageable. You have time (3 days before first funder call).

---

## If Attorney Says "This Is Fine"

Proceed to funder calls with confidence. You've locked in the legal structure.
