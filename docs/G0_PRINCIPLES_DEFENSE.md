# Principles Defense Playbook

**What to do if a funder asks you to compromise on Stewardship Commitment**

This document is your shield. Use it to protect Daanaa's principles when funders pressure you.

---

## The Core Rule

**If a funder asks you to compromise a principle, they're not the right partner.**

Period. Don't negotiate. Don't rationalize. Walk.

The Stewardship Commitment is not a suggestion or a "we try to" document. It's your binding principle. Funders who get it, sign it. Funders who want exceptions don't get funded.

---

## Common Pressure Points (And How to Respond)

### **Pressure #1: "Can you rank organizations for us?"**

**They're asking:** To create public rankings or tier-ings (implicit: "who is best?")

**The principle at stake:** Principle 4 (Small orgs deserve fairness) + Principle 5 (Don't weaponize transparency)

**Your response:**
> "No. We benchmark orgs against their peer group (same size, same NTEE category). That's fairness. Public rankings create shame and competition that hurts small orgs. We're committed to fairness, not hierarchy."

**If they push:** "This is foundational to our model. If you need rankings, we're not the right fit."

---

### **Pressure #2: "Can we get early access to donor data?"**

**They're asking:** Donor names, giving patterns, demographics

**The principle at stake:** Principle 2 (Privacy is structural)

**Your response:**
> "Absolutely not. Donor privacy is non-negotiable. We don't collect giving transaction data, we don't track behavior, and we never share identifying information. That's the whole model."

**If they push:** "This is why donors trust us. If you need donor data, we can't partner."

---

### **Pressure #3: "Can you feature our grantees prominently?"**

**They're asking:** Paid/sponsored placement for orgs they fund

**The principle at stake:** Principle 7 (Independence is protected) + Principle 3 (Trust signals are evidence-based)

**Your response:**
> "No. All orgs are ranked by the same algorithm (peer financial context). Your funding is great, but it doesn't buy visibility on Daanaa. That would break our independence and make trust signals meaningless."

**If they push:** "If you want visibility for grantees, help them claim their profiles and improve their data. That earns better ranking legitimately."

---

### **Pressure #4: "Can you adjust the score methodology for our theory of change?"**

**They're asking:** To tweak scoring to favor certain types of orgs or outcomes

**The principle at stake:** Principle 3 (Trust signals are evidence-based) + Principle 1 (Mission before growth)

**Your response:**
> "No. The scoring methodology is deterministic: it's based on public IRS data and peer benchmarking. We don't adjust it for any funder's preferences. That's the whole point — it's independent and transparent."

**If they want input:** "We welcome research input and feedback, but the final methodology stays independent. We publish it publicly so anyone can audit it."

---

### **Pressure #5: "Can we get equity in Daanaa?"**

**They're asking:** For ownership stake in exchange for funding

**The principle at stake:** Principle 7 (Independence is protected) + Principle 1 (Mission before growth)

**Your response:**
> "No. Daanaa is structured as a DBA of EcoMargins LLC. We're not taking equity partners. You can be an advisor and a funder, but not an owner. That's how we protect independence."

**If they push:** "Equity creates conflicts of interest. We want partners who care about principles, not ROI."

---

### **Pressure #6: "Can you help us with donor acquisition?"**

**They're asking:** To use Daanaa as a marketing channel or lead generation tool

**The principle at stake:** Principle 2 (Privacy is structural) + Principle 5 (Don't weaponize transparency)

**Your response:**
> "No. Daanaa is a directory, not a marketing platform. We don't sell access to donors or nonprofits, and we don't use our platform for partner promotion."

**If they push:** "If you want to reach donors interested in giving, they can find your nonprofit on Daanaa. That's the exchange."

---

### **Pressure #7: "Can you hide or suppress a nonprofit's profile?"**

**They're asking:** To de-rank a nonprofit they don't like or compete with

**The principle at stake:** Principle 4 (Small orgs deserve fairness) + Principle 6 (Mistakes are corrected, not covered up)

**Your response:**
> "Only if there's a data error or the org is not actually a valid 501(c)(3). Otherwise, no. All orgs get equal algorithmic treatment."

**If they push:** "This is the core of our trust model. Suppressing orgs for political reasons would break it."

---

## How to Recognize a "Red Flag" Funder

If they ask you to:

| Red Flag | Translation |
|----------|-------------|
| "Adjust the methodology" | They want to control the algorithm |
| "Feature our grantees" | They want paid placement |
| "Give us donor data" | They want to track people |
| "Hide that nonprofit" | They want to manipulate visibility |
| "Take equity" | They want control |
| "Align with our goals over principles" | They want you to compromise |

**If you hear any of these: Thank them, say you're not aligned, and move on.**

---

## The Conversation You Might Have

**Funder:** "We love Daanaa, but we need [compromise]. Can you work with us?"

**You (your response):**
> "I appreciate that. I'm going to be direct: The Stewardship Commitment isn't negotiable. It's why we exist. If [compromise] conflicts with our principles, we can't do it.
>
> Here's what I can do: [suggest an alternative that honors both principles and their legitimate need]
>
> If that doesn't work, I respect it. But we're not going to compromise on these core principles. That's our moat."

## Principles That ARE Flexible (And Which Aren't)

### **These Are Non-Negotiable:**
1. ✅ Donor privacy (Principle 2)
2. ✅ Evidence-based trust signals (Principle 3)
3. ✅ Independence (Principle 7)
4. ✅ Fairness to small orgs (Principle 4)
5. ✅ Honest communication (Principle 5)

### **These Can Be Discussed:**
- How the partnership is structured
- Budget allocation and timeline
- How they're publicly recognized
- Research/case study participation
- Advisory scope and frequency

## If You Accidentally Compromise (And Realize It Later)

**This can happen.** You might agree to something in a conversation and later realize it violates a principle.

**What to do:**

1. **Stop immediately.** Don't proceed.
2. **Call the funder.** "I've been thinking about our conversation. I said I'd [compromise], but that actually violates a core principle of ours. I need to take that off the table."
3. **Propose an alternative.** "Here's what I can do instead..."
4. **If they push back:** "I appreciate the partnership opportunity, but I have to protect these principles. If this is a dealbreaker for you, I understand."

This is **harder in the moment**, but it's better than slowly drifting away from your values.

---

## Your Power Position

You have leverage because:

1. **You're self-funded.** You don't need this money desperately.
2. **Your principles are your product.** If you compromise, Daanaa stops being differentiated.
3. **Good funders want principled partners.** They respect founders who don't compromise.
4. **You're solving a real problem.** The sector NEEDS what you're building.

Use that leverage. Don't take bad partnerships for capital.

---

## The Test

Here's how to know if a partnership is right:

**Ask yourself:**
- Would I sign the Stewardship Commitment in their position?
- Do they care about principles or just outcomes?
- Would they pressure me to compromise?
- Do they trust my judgment?

If the answer is **yes** to the first three and **yes** to the fourth, they're good partner material.

---

## Reminder

Your principles aren't limiting you. They're protecting you. Funders who align with those principles will be stronger partners than funders who don't.

**Recruit believers. Not compromisers.**
