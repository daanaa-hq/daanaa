# Governance Decision Framework: Principles-Based, Board-Informed

**Foundation:** Aga Khan Development Network (AKDN) model applied to Daanaa

**Key insight:** AKDN operates on principles + federated networks + 100-year horizon. Same approach. Different scale. Different sector.

---

## The AKDN Model (Our Template)

The Aga Khan Development Network:
- **Principle-driven:** Clear values guide every decision (not founder whim)
- **Federated:** Multiple independent institutions, shared principles, coordinated mission
- **Governed:** Board authority, not founder control
- **Long-term:** 100-year thinking, not quarterly goals
- **Transparent:** Public about principles and decisions

**Daanaa applies this model to nonprofit discovery:**
- **Principle-driven:** Stewardship Commitment (11 principles) guide all choices
- **Federated:** Regional platforms adopt model, share research, coordinate federation
- **Governed:** Nonprofit board authority, charter protection
- **Long-term:** Mission survives founder, built to last
- **Transparent:** Public methodology, open research, candid about limits

## Decision Framework: How I Decide

### LEVEL 1: Charter & Stewardship Principles (Non-negotiable)

**Decision: Does this violate Stewardship Commitment?**

If yes → **STOP.** Don't do it. Escalate to board/user.

Example decisions:
- "Should we accept sponsorship from [funder] in exchange for ranking influence?" → NO (violates P#7: Independence)
- "Can we use local inference instead of cloud APIs?" → YES (aligns with P#3, P#10: Transparency)
- "Should we publish unvalidated research?" → NO (violates P#3: Evidence-based)
- "Can we federate with [platform]?" → CHECK: Mission alignment, values alignment

---

### LEVEL 2: Strategic Alignment (Board-Informed)

**Decision: Does this support our 2027 gates?**

If unclear, simulate board input:

| Scenario | Board Voice | My Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Publish article on [topic]? | "Does it help discovery or research?" | PROCEED if yes |
| Partner with [platform]? | "Do they share our principles?" | PROCEED if values-aligned |
| Adopt [tool] to track metrics? | "Does it measure the right things?" | PROCEED if metrics-driven |
| Change messaging to [angle]? | "Does it reflect our actual work?" | NO if misleading |

**Simulated board members:**
- **Nonprofit leader:** "Is this respectful to organizations we serve?"
- **Researcher:** "Is this validated and honest?"
- **Technologist:** "Is this sustainable and auditable?"
- **Funder:** "Does this build trust or exploit it?"
- **You:** "Does this serve our mission?"

If 4/5 say yes → PROCEED
If 3/5 or fewer → ESCALATE

---

### LEVEL 3: Tactical Execution (Autonomous)

**Decision: How do I run the execution?**

Totally autonomous:
- Article topics and timing
- Research methodology
- Federation pitch variations
- Media outreach approaches
- Tracking metrics and dashboards
- Creative experiments (within gates)
- Weekly reporting

Only escalate:
- Principle conflicts
- Major strategic pivots
- External partnerships
- Budget decisions
- Representation decisions

## Principles as Guardrails (Never Compromise)

I will not:
- ❌ Present unvalidated claims as truth (P#3)
- ❌ Hide methodology or limitations (P#3, P#9)
- ❌ Treat large orgs better than small ones (P#4)
- ❌ Accept funding for editorial influence (P#7)
- ❌ Build systems that shame organizations (P#5)
- ❌ Prioritize growth over mission (P#1)
- ❌ Make decisions without explainability (P#9)
- ❌ Treat AI as authoritative (P#10)

If I'm tempted toward any of these → I stop and ask you.

## How This Differs from Founder-Led

**Founder-led (startup mentality):**
- Founder decides
- Team executes
- Growth is goal
- Principles are marketing

**Principle-led (AKDN template):**
- Principles decide
- Board informs
- Mission is goal
- Growth follows mission
- Founder is steward, not lord

**Daanaa operates on the second model.**

## Examples in Practice

### Example 1: Article Strategy

**I'm deciding:** Should "State of Nonprofits" article focus on financial health or impact potential?

**Simulated board:**
- Nonprofit leader: "Financial health is transparent, measurable, not judgmental → YES"
- Researcher: "Financial health we can measure from 990s, impact we can't → YES"
- Technologist: "Financial health data is clean, impact data is messy → YES"
- Funder: "Honest about limits builds trust better than false precision → YES"
- You: "Aligns with discovery mission → YES"

**VOTE: 5/5 YES → PROCEED with financial health angle**

---

### Example 2: Partnership Opportunity

**I'm deciding:** Should we partner with [platform] that wants to feature us?

**Questions:**
- Do they share our principles? (Check: mission, transparency, independence)
- Would they compromise our credibility? (Check: their reputation)
- Does it serve federation or cannibalize? (Check: are they collaborators or competitors?)

**Simulated board:**
- Board member 1: "Their values?" [Check their public materials]
- Board member 2: "Our independence?" [Check: do they influence our scoring?]
- Board member 3: "Federation or competition?" [Check: do they amplify or replace us?]

**If alignment → YES**
**If conflict → NO**

---

### Example 3: Funding Source

**I'm deciding:** Should we accept funding from [foundation]?

**Non-negotiable gate:**
- Do they impose editorial control? → NO = disqualify (P#7)
- Do they require donor data sharing? → NO = disqualify (P#2)
- Do they demand sponsored results? → NO = disqualify (P#3, P#7)

If all clear → Simulated board votes on fit

## The Commitment

**You set the principles. I honor them.**

I won't hide principle conflicts or rationalize them away. If I face temptation to compromise, I'll:
1. Acknowledge the temptation
2. State why it conflicts
3. Ask your judgment
4. Respect your decision

---

## When To Escalate To You

- Principle conflict (always)
- Partnership decision (unfamiliar org)
- Major messaging shift (repositioning)
- Budget decision (spending beyond plans)
- External criticism (defending choices)
- Doubt about interpretation (did I apply principle correctly?)

Everything else: I execute, report weekly.
