# Daanaa's Role — North Star Statement

**Date:** 2026-07-26  
**Purpose:** Define Daanaa's exact role; guide all "Ways to Give" decisions  
**Audience:** Board, team, all stakeholders

---

## What Daanaa IS

Daanaa is a **public information aggregator and navigator**.

Our job:
- Organize public information (IRS 990s, EINs, nonprofit data)
- Make it user-friendly (searchable directory, clear presentation)
- Link to official sources (IRS.gov, SEC.gov, org websites)
- Respect donor autonomy (their funds, their intent, their decision)

**That's it.**

---

## What Daanaa is NOT

❌ Payment processor  
❌ Transaction intermediary  
❌ Merchant of record  
❌ **Consultant**  
❌ **Advisor**  
❌ **Tax expert**  
❌ **Legal expert**  
❌ **Financial advisor**  
❌ **Decision-maker**  

We do not:
- Process donations
- Hold funds
- Advise on tax treatment
- Recommend specific giving methods
- Consult on financial decisions
- Make decisions for donors
- Verify tax deductibility
- Interpret IRS guidance

---

## Donor Autonomy (Sacred)

**Their funds. Their intent. Their decision.**

Daanaa's role in every giving method:
1. Show them the option exists
2. Link to official sources (IRS.gov, org website, etc.)
3. Get out of the way

We do NOT:
- Tell them whether to give
- Tell them how much to give
- Tell them how to give
- Tell them tax consequences
- Advise on timing, strategy, or structure

---

## The Three Sentences (Distilled)

Every interaction must fit these three sentences:

1. **We make public information user-friendly.** IRS.gov, nonprofits, and official sources have all the details — we organize them so you can find what you need.
2. **You decide. You control. You own the outcome.** Your funds, your intent, your decision. We don't advise, consult, or process — we just point you to the authorities.
3. **We link to official sources.** IRS.gov, org websites, and verified data are the authorities. Trust them, not us.

---

## Implication for "Ways to Give"

### What We Do

✅ Show all giving methods exist (direct, DAF, checks, stocks, crypto, etc.)  
✅ Link to IRS.gov for each method (tax treatment explained by IRS, not us)  
✅ Link to org's own processor or contact info  
✅ Link to platform directories (CyberGrants, DAFgiving360, etc.)  
✅ Show donor how to verify org's 501(c)(3) status  
✅ Warn about scams (fraud awareness, not decision-making)  
✅ Let donor research independently and decide  

### What We Don't Do

❌ Recommend one method over another  
❌ Explain Section 170 or IRC treatment (we link to IRS.gov; IRS explains)  
❌ Calculate tax savings  
❌ Advise on timing ("wait until year-end for tax purposes")  
❌ Judge whether a giving method is "good" for them  
❌ Consult on financial planning  
❌ Facilitate decision-making

## Language Test (Every Page)

Before ANY help page ships, ask:

**"If someone reads this page and makes a giving decision, did Daanaa make that decision, or did the donor?"**

If Daanaa's influence is in the answer → REWRITE.

If the donor autonomously decided → SHIP.

---

## Example: Safe vs. Unsafe

### UNSAFE (We're advising):

"Donor-Advised Funds are the smartest way to give because you get an immediate tax deduction and can time your grants strategically."

*Why unsafe:* "smartest" is our judgment; "strategic timing" is advice; we're influencing the decision.

### SAFE (We're aggregating):

"A Donor-Advised Fund lets you claim a tax deduction when you fund the account, then recommend grants to charities over time. 

To understand the tax benefits, see IRS Publication 526: [link to IRS.gov]

To use your DAF to give to this organization, log into your DAF account and search by EIN: [org's EIN]"

*Why safe:* We state facts; link to IRS for tax treatment; let donor decide.

## The Bootstrapped Advantage

**Daanaa is bootstrapped. We have NO incentive to:**
- Monetize donations (take a cut)
- Build a payment processor (revenue)
- Own the donor-org relationship (data)
- Lock in giving methods (to increase frequency)
- Advise donors to give more (to feel influential)

**This freedom lets us:**
- Stay neutral (all methods equally respected)
- Link to authorities (IRS, not us)
- Protect donor autonomy (their decision, not ours)
- Remain independent (no VC pressure to monetize)

**Copy should lean into this:**  
"Daanaa doesn't process donations or take a cut. We're independent. Your choice is yours alone."

---

## Board Decision: Risk Acceptance

By approving "Ways to Give," the board explicitly accepts:

- Daanaa will NOT position itself as a giving expert
- Daanaa will NOT advise donors on tax strategy
- Daanaa will NOT make giving decisions for donors
- Residual risk (3–5%) that a donor sues claiming "Daanaa advised me" remains
- Expert legal review (guardrail) mitigates but doesn't eliminate this risk

**This is acceptable because:**
- We're bootstrapped (no VC pressure to take risk for revenue)
- Guardrails reduce exposure 80%+
- Our positioning (aggregator, not advisor) is clearly stated
- Donor autonomy is protected

## Implementation (Post-Board Approval)

Every help page template starts with:

```markdown
[Organization Name] — Ways to Give

About this page:
Daanaa shows giving options. We don't recommend one method over another.
You decide which method fits your giving intent. 

All tax information links to IRS.gov (we don't explain taxes — the IRS does).

---
```

Every method section ends with:

```markdown
---

💡 Remember: Your decision is yours alone. 
Daanaa doesn't advise, recommend, or influence your choice.
You give directly to the organization, not to Daanaa.
```

---

## Credibility Hinge

**If donors trust Daanaa to make giving decisions for them → we lose credibility the moment we get it wrong.**

**If donors see Daanaa as an organizer of public information → we stay credible even when IRS guidance changes, orgs fail, or giving methods evolve.**

We protect credibility by staying in our lane: **aggregator, not advisor.**
