# T2 Attorney Prep: Nonprofit Group Purchasing Organization (GPO) Entity Sketch

**Purpose:** Legal structure for Daanaa's second business line (group purchasing) without conflating it with the free discovery platform.  
**For:** Attorney to refine the entity model before launch (gated at G3: 100+ claimed orgs).  
**Context:** After Daanaa free is live + letter service is live, explore a "Nonprofit GPO" that negotiates volume discounts (software, insurance, supplies) and takes a commission on savings.

---

## The Business Model (G3+ launch)

**What:** Daanaa GPO negotiates contracts with vendors (SalesForce, QuickBooks, etc.) to offer member nonprofits volume discounts.

**How:** 
1. Nonprofit joins GPO (free or small membership fee)
2. GPO publishes negotiated rates/contracts (SalesForce -15%, QuickBooks Premium -20%, etc.)
3. Nonprofit buys directly from vendor using the GPO code; vendor tracks & validates membership
4. **GPO revenue model:** Take 5–10% of *savings* (not revenue), or flat annual fee per member
5. Example: 500 nonprofits × $500/year avg savings × 7% = **$1.75M in direct revenue** (at scale)

**Why it works for Daanaa:**
- Leverages existing nonprofit database + member trust (Daanaa's discovery platform is a natural on-ramp)
- Complements free discovery (discovery → letter service → group purchasing = deepening engagement)
- Daanaa doesn't handle funds (vendor processes billing directly)
- Recurring revenue stream (annual or per-transaction)

## Legal Entity Options

### Option 1: Daanaa 501(c)(3) Operates GPO Directly
**Structure:** Daanaa Inc. (501(c)(3)) has a "GPO division" (unrelated business income — taxable, but allowed).

**Pros:**
- Simple; no new entity
- Tax-exempt parent can reinvest GPO revenue into free discovery mission
- Members trust "Daanaa" brand

**Cons:**
- GPO revenue is "unrelated business income" (UBI) — Daanaa must file Form 990-T (tax return for UBI)
- Threshold: UBI > $1K/year triggers Form 990-T filing (~$300–500 cost annually)
- If GPO grows large, tax burden could offset savings
- Mission creep: hard to separate "discovery" (tax-exempt) from "group purchasing" (taxable)

**Legal requirement:** Daanaa 501(c)(3) bylaws must allow "unrelated business income"; most standard bylaws do.

### Option 2: Subsidiary 501(c)(3) GPO (Controlled by Daanaa)
**Structure:** Daanaa Discovery 501(c)(3) + Daanaa GPO 501(c)(3) (both subsidiary to a parent holding company, or separately with cross-board seats).

**Pros:**
- Clean separation: discovery is tax-exempt, GPO is tax-exempt (IF it qualifies)
- GPO *could* be tax-exempt if it's a "cooperative" or operates for mutual benefit of members
- Two profit centers, separate financials (clearer to funders + donors)
- GPO can take on modest debt / VC if needed (separate 501(c)(3) doesn't hurt donor optics for Daanaa)

**Cons:**
- Two 501(c)(3) applications (4–12 months)
- Two annual Form 990 filings (~$1–2K accounting cost/year)
- GPO 501(c)(3) status is not guaranteed (IRS scrutinizes whether it's truly charitable or just a member benefit org)
- More complex governance (two boards, shared services, inter-org agreements)

### Option 3: For-Profit GPO Subsidiary (Under EcoMargins or new LLC)
**Structure:** Daanaa Discovery 501(c)(3) + EcoMargins LLC (which owns a For-Profit GPO LLC).

**Pros:**
- GPO operates as a standard LLC (no tax complexity, no Form 990-T)
- EcoMargins revenue increases (can fund Daanaa discovery as a grant/donation)
- Cleanest separation of charitable + commercial
- For-profit GPO can take VC, credit lines, etc.

**Cons:**
- If EcoMargins owns the GPO, Daanaa 501(c)(3) can't control it (creates tax-exempt support for for-profit issue)
- Donors may hesitate: "Is my donation funding the free platform or the for-profit GPO?"
- IRS scrutiny: "Why is Daanaa 501(c)(3) referring to a for-profit GPO?" (though legal, it looks fishy)
- Revenue leakage: GPO profits don't fund Daanaa directly; they fund EcoMargins (which then decides whether to grant back)

## Recommended Structure (for attorney to refine)

**Hybrid: Daanaa Discovery 501(c)(3) + Daanaa GPO 501(c)(3) under a parent holding company (optional parent, or cross-board governance)**

**Rationale:**
- Both entities can be 501(c)(3) if GPO qualifies as a cooperative or mutual-benefit org
- Separation avoids UBI tax complexity (if GPO doesn't trigger UBI, or UBI is minimal)
- Both serve the same mission: supporting nonprofits
- At scale, if GPO revenue is substantial, you can spin it to EcoMargins (for-profit) without major restructuring

**Governance:**
- Shared executive director (Akbar, later replaced)
- Two separate boards (overlapping membership OK)
- Formal "service agreement" between them (Daanaa GPO provides services to Daanaa Discovery at cost, or small fee)

**Timeline:**
- **Now (G0):** File Daanaa Discovery 501(c)(3) + mention GPO in the mission statement ("supports nonprofits through discovery, letter services, and group purchasing partnerships")
- **G3 (100+ claimed orgs):** File Daanaa GPO 501(c)(3) separately (IRS is more receptive once you have proof of nonprofit engagement)
- **G3+:** Launch GPO as a separate offering (member sign-up, vendor negotiations, reporting)

## Vendor Negotiation & Legal Risk

**Daanaa's role:**
- You negotiate contracts on behalf of members (does NOT make you a broker or intermediary in the legal sense, if structured correctly)
- You publish rates, but member buys directly from vendor
- You don't handle funds; vendor bills member directly

**Insurance & liability:**
- Daanaa GPO needs E&O insurance (errors & omissions): $2–5K/year
- Vendor contracts must disclaim liability: "Daanaa GPO does not endorse or warrant vendor performance"
- Member agreement must include: "You bear all risk of vendor performance; Daanaa is not liable for vendor failures"

**Vendor compliance:**
- Vendors may ask: "Who signs the contract?" (Answer: Daanaa GPO as a 501(c)(3), or EcoMargins as for-profit, depending on structure)
- W-9 / tax ID: Daanaa GPO will need its own EIN (IRS), even if subsidiary to holding company

## IRS / Tax Considerations

**If Daanaa GPO is 501(c)(3):**
- Must file Form 1023 (same as discovery)
- Form 990 annual filing (report GPO revenue + expenses)
- UBI threshold: If GPO revenue is <$1K/year, you skip Form 990-T. At scale, you *may* owe UBI tax (depends on IRS guidance on cooperatives)
- **Key question for attorney:** Can a nonprofit GPO be structured as a "cooperative" to avoid UBI tax? (IRS has issued guidance saying yes, under certain conditions)

**If Daanaa GPO is for-profit (LLC):**
- EcoMargins LLC owns it; no separate 501(c)(3)
- EcoMargins pays corporate tax on GPO profits
- Simple from a tax perspective, but complicates optics (Daanaa 501(c)(3) supporting a for-profit subsidiary)

## Questions for Attorney

1. **Can a 501(c)(3) GPO avoid Form 990-T if it's structured as a member cooperative?** (If yes, this is the cleanest path.)
2. **If we file Daanaa GPO as a separate 501(c)(3), can we set it up as a "controlled subsidiary" of Daanaa Discovery without a parent holding company?** (Simpler governance.)
3. **Vendor contracts:** Should Daanaa GPO (nonprofit) or EcoMargins (for-profit) sign vendor agreements? (Impact on vendor confidence + tax treatment.)
4. **Inter-org agreement:** If Daanaa Discovery refers members to Daanaa GPO, does that create a taxable "related organization" issue? (Should be OK, but confirm.)
5. **Insurance:** What E&O policy limit should Daanaa GPO carry for vendor disputes?
